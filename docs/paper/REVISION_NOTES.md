# NLTopt manuscript — revision notes (2026-10-14)

The manuscript was restructured from a catalog of results into a hypothesis-driven paper.
`nltopt_paper_v1_archive.tex` is the previous version, kept verbatim.

## Structure
- **Question:** can the *spatial distribution* of fiber orientation reduce geometric hardening at
  fixed linear frequency and mass; what is the mechanism, what bounds it, and how does it compare
  with a uniform angle and with stacking?
- **Hypothesis:** frequency = ∫κᵀDκ, hardening γ = ½∫ε_rᵀAε_r. The two strain fields differ in
  magnitude and principal direction from point to point, so a field can satisfy the trade-off
  pointwise, while a uniform angle can only satisfy it on average.
- **Predictions P1–P5**, each tested in Sec. 5. There is a verdict table in Sec. 6.
- Files: `nltopt_paper.tex` (main), `sec_*.tex`, `numbers.tex` (auto-generated by
  `examples/paper_numbers.py`; every quoted number), `nltopt_extensions.tex` (companion notes:
  prescribed backbones, density field, flutter — old model, must be re-verified).

## Corrections to claims in the previous draft
1. **Beam mechanism.** The previous draft attributed the beam lever to bending–extension coupling
   B (5%). Re-run with the corrected SSM, that design gives ~2%. Symmetric steering (B ≡ 0) with
   compliant fibers at the inflection points gives −19% (900°/m tow limit) and up to −60% (no limit).
   Closed form: J ∝ 1/(⟨E⁻¹⟩·⟨Eφ''²⟩).
2. **SSM bug.** The QZ master eigenvector of the stiff first-order pencil lost ~10 digits for
   laminated beams at some angles (γ was 10× wrong at 45°). It now uses Rayleigh-quotient inverse
   iteration with u₁ = conj(v₁) for symmetric systems. A regression test was added.
3. **Basis convergence.** The 3×3 in-plane basis under-resolved the relaxation of steered plates
   (34% vs. a converged 51%). The headline design was re-optimized on the converged
   8×8 transverse / 6×6 in-plane basis, at an exact frequency equality: −63% (was −50% at +5%
   frequency).
4. **Baselines.** UD is the lowest-frequency member of all uniform stacks, so at f_UD there is no
   uniform competitor. At higher frequencies the steered designs beat the best stacks
   (0.29/0.24/0.21 vs. 0.81/0.62/0.56 for the 15° grid; superseded in figure round 2 by the continuous-stack optimum 0.74/0.61/0.55).
5. The claim that "no classical knob decouples nonlinear stiffness" was replaced by a measured
   comparison with stacking.
6. Inconsistent numbers (−21/256 "within 5%" vs. "exact"; the mislabeled verification row;
   17.1% vs. 10.5% baselines) were removed. All numbers are now generated.
