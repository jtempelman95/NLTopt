\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What is proved, what is computed, and what is not established}
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Proved} (for the stated model classes). Orientation enters each energy density only
  through $U_2,U_3$ and the local principal directions (Proposition~\ref{prop:fourier}). Isotropic
  material has no lever (Corollary~\ref{cor:iso}). A single-ply beam's $J$ is invariant under any
  uniform angle (Corollary~\ref{cor:beaminv}). The lowest-frequency uniform symmetric laminate is a
  single-angle one (Corollary~\ref{cor:udfloor}). A uniform angle that violates
  \eqref{eq:uniformkkt} is not stationary among fields in the frozen-field model
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:pointwise}), and the jump-free amplitude scales as $J^{-1/2}$ at the cubic
  order (Proposition~\ref{prop:cusp}).
\item \textbf{Computed}, with the stated discretizations and optimizer. The size of every gain:
  $J/J_{\UD}=\nFTen$ at held frequency (between $\nFEmatchedJ$ and $\nFTen$ with the frequency re-matched in
  refined and independent models), the margin over the surveyed uniform classes above
  $\omega_{1,\UD}$, the dose--response in anisotropy and tow radius, the generality across plates and
  its ordering by the conflict $\chi$, and the as-built sensitivity. These are best-found values of a
  local optimizer (Table~\ref{tab:ms}). The steered values are therefore upper bounds on the attainable
  hardening, and the uniform values above $\omega_{1,\UD}$ are upper bounds on the best uniform laminate.
\item \textbf{Not established.} That the reported fields are global optima. That no uniform laminate
  outside the surveyed classes (more plies, a lamination-parameter search) does better above
  $\omega_{1,\UD}$; the steered values are at most $\nSteerVsUniformMax\%$ of the present uniform
  optimum. Performance of a physical panel.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Relation to prior work}
The tools are not new: SSM reduction and adjoint SSM sensitivities
\cite{haller2016,jain2022,li2024,pozzi2025,liang2025}, lamination parameters \cite{tsai1968}, and
curvature-constrained orientation fields \cite{wong2026}. What is new is their target and the
explanation. Fiber paths have been optimized for the linear frequency, and the nonlinear response of
the optimum was then examined \cite{farsadi2021,farsadi2025}. The nonlinear free, forced and thermal
vibrations of prescribed curvilinear-fiber plates have been computed
\cite{ribeiro2012,akhavan2015,akhavan2017,houmat2013,yan2021,zhao2026}. Here the hardening is the
objective, the linear frequency an equality constraint, and the result a mechanism with falsifiable
predictions. On the clamped beam, the operative lever is not the bending--extension coupling but the
harmonic-versus-curvature-weighted mean of Proposition~\ref{prop:beam}, which needs no coupling at
all. The relaxed-stretch mechanism parallels the load-redistribution explanation of steered
postbuckling stiffness \cite{gurdal,wu2013}.

\subsection{Limitations and scope}
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Buildability.} The designs are curvature-feasible idealized tow fields. The curvature limit
  holds pointwise (radius $\ge\nRminFine$\,mm on an independent grid), but the field spreads tows by more
  than a decade (Sec.~\ref{sec:asbuilt}). Laying it requires course-level tow cut/add, and the as-built
  model shifts frequency and mass by several percent. A buildable design at exactly held frequency would
  include a thickness/defect field tied to $\theta(\bm x)$ in the optimization. The defect model here is a
  continuum estimate; ply-level gap/overlap geometry \cite{fayazbakhsh2013,pagani2025}, tow width and
  coverage strategy, and process effects (wrinkling, resin-rich zones) are not resolved.
\item \textbf{Laminate.} The plate designs are one steered layer, with $\bm B=\bm 0$ but unbalanced
  ($A_{16}\neq0$). A multi-ply laminate with balanced, independently steered layers is not studied.
  The beam design with no tow limit turns to about $\nBeamMaxAngle^\circ$ in narrow zones of the
  $20$\,mm-wide strip.
\item \textbf{Amplitude range.} The design quantity is the small-amplitude cubic coefficient. The cubic
  SSM reproduces the full-order EPMC detuning to within $\nEPMCAgree\%$ over the amplitudes EPMC reached
  ($a/h\le\nEPMCmaxUD$ for UD, $\le\nEPMCmaxOpt$ for the steered design), and the jump-onset amplitudes
  reported lie inside these ranges. Higher amplitudes need the quintic SSM or EPMC
  (Appendix~\ref{app:calib}).
\item \textbf{Single mode, no internal resonance.} On the full multi-mode plate the steered design's
  lowest frequencies are in ratios $\nModeRatios$ (\texttt{mode\_\allowbreak ratios.py}). The nearest
  commensurability ($3{:}1$ with the third mode) is detuned by $\nDetThreeOne\%$, and that mode is
  antisymmetric about the center. The six-mode modal time-integration model contains these modes
  and would show an internal-resonance exchange; its sweeps follow the single-mode forced
  response instead (Fig.~\ref{fig:confirm}b,c).
\item \textbf{Quasi-static in-plane field.} The relaxed cubic slaves the in-plane field statically.
  Proposition~\ref{prop:ssmcond} bounds the error by $4\omega_1^2/\omega_{\mathrm{in}}^2=\nRhoIn$, and
  the full SSM, which retains in-plane inertia, agrees to $\nSSMvsEval\%$.
\item \textbf{Optimality.} The optimizer is local (multi-start SLSQP); two of six starts stall
  (Table~\ref{tab:ms}). The pointwise ceiling of Sec.~\ref{sec:limits} bounds what any single-ply field
  can do on this plate.
\item \textbf{Scope of the robustness study.} Five plates, one material, one thickness ($a/h=100$, thin
  von K\'arm\'an), and the fundamental mode only. Thick plates, shells, other modes and damping that is
  itself designed are not studied.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Outlook}
The same reduction and adjoint carry over directly to three extensions:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt]
\item \emph{prescribing} the backbone (a target frequency--amplitude law) instead of
  minimizing the hardening;
\item designing tow path and thickness/defect field together, so that a buildable laminate holds the
  frequency exactly;
\item steering stability boundaries, such as supersonic panel flutter, whose
  nonsymmetric aeroelastic operator enters the same first-order pencil; the flutter and
  post-flutter response of plates with prescribed curvilinear fibers has been analyzed
  \cite{akhavan2018,akhavan2020,farsadi2019}, but not designed.
\end{itemize}
The mechanism of Sec.~\ref{sec:theory} predicts where each extension should help: wherever
two functionals of the same stiffness are weighted by different strain fields, in proportion to
the conflict between those fields.

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}
The frequency and the geometric hardening of a laminated panel integrate the same rotated stiffness
against two strain fields of the fundamental, the curvature and the relaxed stretch. Each density
reads the fiber angle only through the anisotropic invariants and the local principal directions.
Where the two fields disagree, a spatial fiber field can serve both and a uniform layup cannot. On a
clamped beam this means compliant fibers at the inflection points. On a simply supported plate, at the
unidirectional frequency and mass, it reduces the hardening by about two thirds in every model we
checked. No uniform laminate is available at that frequency, and above it steering undercuts every
uniform class surveyed. The lever vanishes for isotropic material, grows with anisotropy and with the
conflict between the fields, and saturates once tows can turn on the fields' length scale. Because the
jump-free amplitude scales as $J^{-1/2}$, the gain appears directly as a $\nCacRatio\times$ wider
hysteresis-free range, independent of the damping level. Turning the idealized tow fields into buildable
laminates requires tow cut/add, which our defect model suggests moves the design along, not off, the
steered front.
