composite beams via spectral submanifolds}
\author{J.\,R.\ Tempelman}
\date{Working draft --- \today}

\begin{abstract}
Reduced-order models on spectral submanifolds (SSMs) have recently made it
practical to \emph{optimize} the nonlinear dynamic response of structures --- the
backbone and forced-response curves --- because both the response and its
sensitivity are available analytically. To date that machinery has been driven
by \emph{shape}, \emph{size}, and \emph{density}-topology design variables. We
introduce a different, and for composites more natural, design variable: the
\emph{fiber-orientation field} (tow path) $\theta(x)$, and we impose the
\emph{tow-curvature manufacturability constraint} that governs automated fiber
placement. We build a laminated von~K\'arm\'an beam whose per-station laminate
stiffnesses $A(\theta),B(\theta),D(\theta)$ follow from classical laminate
theory, reduce its first mode on an arbitrary-order 2-D SSM, and optimize the
tow path for an \emph{amplitude-robust} (minimally detuning) resonance. The
enabling physics is the laminate bending--extension coupling $B$: a plain
single-angle rotation scales $EI$ and $EA$ together and cannot touch the
hardening, whereas an unsymmetric, \emph{steered} layup injects a tunable
quadratic force that partially cancels the cubic membrane hardening. We verify
the reduction against the analytic Duffing backbone (including the $-21/256$
fifth-order coefficient) and against full-order harmonic-balance/EPMC
continuation, and we reproduce two figures of a recent SSM-optimization paper as
calibration. On a clamped beam the membrane hardening dominates and the coupling
lever is bounded, so at fixed linear frequency a manufacturable tow layout
\emph{reduces} the amplitude detuning only modestly (a few percent) --- a limit
set by the coupling lever, not by manufacturability. We report this honestly and
identify the routes along which the effect grows: a shallow initial curvature as
a softening reserve, and the move to a two-dimensional laminated plate. To our
knowledge this is nonetheless the first design of a composite's \emph{nonlinear}
backbone through a manufacturable fiber-orientation field, and the verified
framework is the contribution.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Nonlinearity in the design phase is increasingly functional rather than
parasitic: frequency division, vibration mitigation, energy harvesting, and
MEMS resonators all exploit amplitude-dependent stiffness
\cite{shaw,touze}. Tailoring that behavior means shaping the
\emph{backbone curve} --- the frequency--amplitude relation of a nonlinear
normal mode. The backbone is classically traced by numerical continuation of
the harmonic-balance or shooting equations, but reduction onto a
\emph{spectral submanifold} (SSM) \cite{haller2016,jain2022} yields the backbone
and, crucially, its parameter sensitivity in closed form, at a cost independent
of the number of design variables when the adjoint method is used.

Three recent works establish SSM/ROM-based optimization of nonlinear dynamics:
Pozzi \emph{et al.}~\cite{pozzi2025} derive adjoint sensitivities of the
SSM backbone and shape a von~K\'arm\'an beam to a prescribed softening target;
Liang \emph{et al.}~\cite{liang2025} optimize nonlinear forced-response curves
(peak amplitude, hardening/softening, saddle-node spacing) via SSM reduction;
and Xiao's thesis~\cite{xiao2026} develops ROM-based optimization of nonlinear
systems broadly. All three parametrize the design by \emph{shape, size, or
element density}. None uses a fiber-orientation field, and none imposes a
manufacturability constraint.

Separately, \emph{variable-stiffness composites} --- laminates whose fiber tows
are steered along curvilinear paths by automated fiber placement --- are a mature
design space, but the objectives pursued there are almost entirely
\emph{linear}: in-plane stiffness, buckling load, and linear natural
frequencies \cite{gurdal,ijsselmuiden}, subject to a tow-curvature (steering
radius) manufacturability limit. The intersection --- steering the tow path to
tailor a composite's \emph{nonlinear} backbone, under manufacturability --- is,
to our knowledge, open. That intersection is the subject of this paper.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item An arbitrary-order 2-D SSM reduction (Sec.~\ref{sec:ssm}) built directly
from a finite-element mechanical system, verified against the analytic Duffing
backbone and against full-order EPMC continuation, and calibrated by reproducing
two figures of \cite{pozzi2025}.
\item A laminated von~K\'arm\'an beam (Sec.~\ref{sec:beam}) whose nonlinear
backbone is a function of the fiber-orientation field, exposing the
bending--extension coupling $B(\theta)$ as the design lever, together with the
observation that it is the fiber \emph{steering} (the spatial gradient of
$\theta$), not merely an unsymmetric layup, that supplies the tunable quadratic
nonlinearity once the axial degrees of freedom are slaved.
\item A manufacturability-constrained optimization of the tow path for an
amplitude-robust resonance (Sec.~\ref{sec:results}), with an honest account of
what the coupling lever can and cannot achieve on a clamped beam.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The nonlinear system and its spectral submanifold}\label{sec:ssm}

\subsection{Second-order form}
We consider a finite-element mechanical system
\begin{equation}
\bm{M}\ddot{\bm{x}} + \bm{C}\dot{\bm{x}} + \bm{K}\bm{x}
 + \bm{f}_{\mathrm{nl}}(\bm{x}) = \bm 0,
\qquad
\bm f_{\mathrm{nl}}(\bm x) = \bm B_2(\bm x,\bm x) + \bm T_3(\bm x,\bm x,\bm x),
\label{eq:eom}
\end{equation}
with symmetric mass, damping and stiffness matrices and displacement-dependent
nonlinearity split into its symmetric quadratic ($\bm B_2$) and cubic ($\bm T_3$)
multilinear forms. In first-order state space $\bm z=[\bm x;\dot{\bm x}]$,
\begin{equation}
\bm{\mathcal B}\dot{\bm z}=\bm{\mathcal A}\bm z+\bm F(\bm z),\quad
\bm{\mathcal B}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm C&\bm M\\\bm M&\bm 0\end{bmatrix},\;
\bm{\mathcal A}=\begin{bmatrix}-\bm K&\bm 0\\\bm 0&\bm M\end{bmatrix},\;
\bm F=\begin{bmatrix}-\bm f_{\mathrm{nl}}(\bm x)\\\bm 0\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:firstorder}
\end{equation}
The master mode $(\omega_0,\bm\varphi)$ solves $(\bm K-\omega_0^2\bm M)\bm\varphi=\bm0$
with $\bm\varphi^{\!\top}\bm M\bm\varphi=1$; its state-space eigenvalue is
$\lambda_1=-\zeta_0\omega_0+i\omega_0\sqrt{1-\zeta_0^2}$ with
$\zeta_0=\bm\varphi^{\!\top}\bm C\bm\varphi/(2\omega_0)$.

\subsection{Invariance equation}
A spectral submanifold $\mathcal W$ is the smoothest invariant manifold of
\eqref{eq:firstorder} tangent, at the origin, to a chosen spectral subspace $E$
of the linearization --- here the two-dimensional eigenspace of the master pair
$\lambda_1,\lambda_2=\bar\lambda_1$ \cite{haller2016,jain2022}. It carries a
single nonlinear mode: trajectories launched on $\mathcal W$ remain on it, so the
system's vector field is everywhere \emph{tangent} to $\mathcal W$. We use this
tangency to compute $\mathcal W$ and the dynamics it carries.

Parametrize $\mathcal W$ by a two-dimensional chart $\bm p=(p_1,p_2)\in\C^2$
through an embedding $\bm W:\C^2\!\to\C^{2N}$ and let the induced (reduced)
dynamics on the chart be $\bm R$:
\begin{equation}
\bm z=\bm W(\bm p),\qquad \dot{\bm p}=\bm R(\bm p).
\label{eq:param}
\end{equation}
Differentiating $\bm z=\bm W(\bm p)$ in time gives
$\dot{\bm z}=\mathrm D\bm W(\bm p)\,\dot{\bm p}=\mathrm D\bm W(\bm p)\,\bm R(\bm p)$;
requiring this to equal the true velocity field~\eqref{eq:firstorder} on the
manifold yields the \textbf{invariance equation}
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;
\bm{\mathcal B}\,\mathrm D\bm W(\bm p)\,\bm R(\bm p)
=\bm{\mathcal A}\,\bm W(\bm p)+\bm F\!\big(\bm W(\bm p)\big)
\;}
\label{eq:invariance}
\end{equation}
a single functional equation in the two unknowns $\bm W$ (the geometry) and
$\bm R$ (the reduced dynamics). Because $E$ is spanned by the master
eigenvectors, we take conjugate coordinates $p_2=\bar p_1$; a real physical
motion is then $\bm x=[\bm I\;\bm0]\bm W(\bm p)$ evaluated on $p_2=\bar p_1$.

\subsection{Solution by a graded power series}
Expand~\eqref{eq:invariance} as power series in $\bm p$,
\begin{equation}
\bm W(\bm p)=\!\!\sum_{|\bm m|\ge1}\!\!\bm w_{\bm m}\bm p^{\bm m},\qquad
\bm R(\bm p)=\!\!\sum_{|\bm m|\ge1}\!\!\bm R_{\bm m}\bm p^{\bm m},\qquad
\bm p^{\bm m}=p_1^{m_1}p_2^{m_2},
\end{equation}
with multi-index $\bm m=(m_1,m_2)$. At first order tangency to $E$ fixes
$\bm w_{\bm e_1}=\bm v_1,\ \bm w_{\bm e_2}=\bm v_2$ (the master right
eigenvectors) and a diagonal linear reduced flow $R^1_{\bm e_1}=\lambda_1,\
R^2_{\bm e_2}=\lambda_2$. Collecting~\eqref{eq:invariance} at a general order
$\bm m$ ($|\bm m|\ge2$) separates the unknown coefficient of that order and gives
the \textbf{homological equation}
\begin{equation}
(\Lambda_{\bm m}\bm{\mathcal B}-\bm{\mathcal A})\,\bm w_{\bm m}
+\bm{\mathcal B}\big(\bm v_1 R^1_{\bm m}+\bm v_2 R^2_{\bm m}\big)
=\bm G_{\bm m},\qquad \Lambda_{\bm m}=m_1\lambda_1+m_2\lambda_2 .
\label{eq:homological}
\end{equation}
The left side is the linearization of~\eqref{eq:invariance} about the master
subspace: the diagonal term $\Lambda_{\bm m}\bm w_{\bm m}$ comes from
$\bm{\mathcal B}\,\mathrm D\bm W\,\bm R$ paired with the linear flow, and the
$\bm v_j R^j_{\bm m}$ term is the contribution of the unknown reduced coefficient
at this order. The right side $\bm G_{\bm m}$ collects everything already known
from \emph{lower} orders: the order-$\bm m$ part of the nonlinear force
$-[\bm F(\bm W)]_{\bm m}$, assembled by the multi-index convolutions
\begin{equation}
[\bm B_2(\bm x,\bm x)]_{\bm m}\!=\!\!\sum_{\bm a+\bm b=\bm m}\!\!
\bm B_2(\bm w^{x}_{\bm a},\bm w^{x}_{\bm b}),\quad
[\bm T_3(\bm x,\bm x,\bm x)]_{\bm m}\!=\!\!\!\sum_{\bm a+\bm b+\bm c=\bm m}\!\!\!
\bm T_3(\bm w^{x}_{\bm a},\bm w^{x}_{\bm b},\bm w^{x}_{\bm c}),
\end{equation}
($\bm w^{x}$ the displacement block of $\bm w$), minus the cross-terms
$\bm{\mathcal B}\sum a_j\,\bm w_{\bm a}R^j_{\bm b}$ generated by
$\mathrm D\bm W\,\bm R$ from lower-order manifold and reduced coefficients. Thus
\eqref{eq:homological} is solved recursively, order by order.

\subsection{Resonances, the reduced dynamics, and the backbone}
The operator $\Lambda_{\bm m}\bm{\mathcal B}-\bm{\mathcal A}$ is singular exactly
when $\Lambda_{\bm m}$ hits an eigenvalue of the pencil $(\bm{\mathcal A},
\bm{\mathcal B})$ --- an (inner) resonance. For the master pair this occurs at the
indices $\bm m=(k{+}1,k)$, where $\Lambda_{(k+1,k)}=\lambda_1$ in the
conservative case (and lies near it under light damping). At these indices one
cannot solve for $\bm w_{\bm m}$ alone; the (near-)singular component is instead
carried by the reduced dynamics. Projecting~\eqref{eq:homological} onto the left
eigenvector $\bm u_1$ (defined by $\bm u_1^{*}\bm{\mathcal A}=\lambda_1
\bm u_1^{*}\bm{\mathcal B}$ and normalized $\bm u_1^{*}\bm{\mathcal B}\bm v_1=1$)
annihilates the singular operator and isolates the reduced coefficient
\begin{equation}
\gamma_{2k+1}\;:=\;R^1_{(k+1,k)}\;=\;\bm u_1^{*}\,\bm G_{(k+1,k)} .
\label{eq:gamma}
\end{equation}
The remaining (now solvable) system fixes $\bm w_{\bm m}$ under the normal-form
gauge $\bm u_1^{*}\bm{\mathcal B}\bm w_{\bm m}=0$, which --- as one verifies by
applying $\bm u_1^{*}$ to the residual --- is met automatically by the solve,
damped or not. Non-resonant indices set $\bm R_{\bm m}=\bm 0$ and invert
$\Lambda_{\bm m}\bm{\mathcal B}-\bm{\mathcal A}$ directly. Equation~\eqref{eq:gamma}
is exactly an \emph{adjoint} projection --- $\bm u_1$ is the adjoint mode --- which
is why the same construction later delivers inexpensive design sensitivities.

Keeping only the resonant terms, the reduced dynamics is
$\dot p_1=\big(\lambda_1+\sum_{k\ge1}\gamma_{2k+1}\,p_1^{k+1}p_2^{k}\big)$. On the
conjugate orbit $p_1=\tfrac{\rho}{2}e^{i\theta},\ p_2=\bar p_1$ one has
$p_1^{k+1}p_2^{k}=|p_1|^{2k}p_1=(\rho/2)^{2k}p_1$, so
$\dot p_1/p_1=\lambda_1+\sum_k\gamma_{2k+1}(\rho/2)^{2k}$ and the \textbf{backbone}
(instantaneous frequency) and modal decay are
\begin{equation}
\Om(\rho)=\Imag\!\Big(\lambda_1+\!\sum_{k\ge1}\gamma_{2k+1}(\rho/2)^{2k}\Big),
\qquad
\alpha(\rho)=\Real\!\Big(\lambda_1+\!\sum_{k\ge1}\gamma_{2k+1}(\rho/2)^{2k}\Big).
\label{eq:backbone}
\end{equation}
The cubic coefficient $\gamma_3$ alone yields a monotone parabola --- hardening if
$\Imag\gamma_3>0$, softening if $<0$; the quintic $\gamma_5$ (order-5 reduction) is
what lets a softening branch turn around and harden. Physical amplitudes on the
backbone follow by sampling $\bm x=[\bm I\;\bm0]\bm W(\bm p)$ over one period.

\paragraph{Consistency and verification.}
The multilinear forms $\bm B_2,\bm T_3$ are extracted from the finite-element
nonlinearity by even/odd decomposition and polarization, so the SSM and a
harmonic-balance model consume \emph{identical} nonlinearities. Table~\ref{tab:verify}
summarizes the verification. For the single-degree-of-freedom Duffing oscillator
$\ddot x+\omega_0^2x+\varepsilon x^3=0$ the reduction reproduces the exact
first-order coefficient and, at fifth order, the analytic fourth-order backbone
coefficient
$\Om/\omega_0=1+\tfrac38\varepsilon A^2-\tfrac{21}{256}\varepsilon^2A^4+\dots$
to within a few percent. Against full-order EPMC continuation of a
quadratically-coupled two-degree-of-freedom system and of a clamped--clamped
von~K\'arm\'an beam, the backbones agree to sub-percent at the amplitudes where
the expansion is converged.

\subsection{Backbone sensitivity and calibration against prior work}
For a small number of design parameters $\bm\mu$ we obtain the backbone
sensitivity $\partial\Om/\partial\bm\mu$ at fixed physical amplitude by finite
differencing the (cheap) SSM backbone; the analytic adjoint of
\cite{pozzi2025,li2024} computes the same quantity at a cost independent of the
number of parameters and is the natural next step for a full tow-path field. As
calibration we reproduce two results of \cite{pozzi2025}. First
(their Fig.~2, Fig.~\ref{fig:fig2} here), the backbone sensitivity of two coupled
oscillators predicts the perturbed backbone, $\Om(\bm\mu+\delta\bm\mu)\approx
\Om(\bm\mu)+\partial_{\bm\mu}\Om\,\delta\bm\mu$, to $\sim\!10^{-5}$ over the
system's soften-then-harden ``backward-C'' backbone (which requires the
fifth-order SSM to capture). Second (their Fig.~3, Fig.~\ref{fig:fig3} here), we
shape an initially curved beam to a prescribed softening target and confirm the
optimized backbone against EPMC to $<0.1\%$ at both target points.
We note two honest departures in the second calibration: we use the
axially-condensed shallow-arch model rather than a full three-DOF-per-node
element, and we place the target amplitudes inside the cubic-SSM's verified
window (the paper's larger target sits beyond it, requiring higher order). We
also observe that Eq.~(33) of \cite{pozzi2025}, taken literally, places a
same-sign quadratic coupling on both masses, which is not energy-consistent; we
use the potential-derived spring, which leaves the reported eigenvalue unchanged.

\section{Fiber-steered laminated von K\'arm\'an beam}\label{sec:beam}

\subsection{Laminate stiffnesses from the tow angle}
For a laminate of plies at angles $\theta_k$ (relative to the beam axis) the
extensional, coupling and bending stiffnesses follow from classical laminate
theory,
\begin{equation}
(A,B,D)=\sum_k \bar{\bm Q}(\theta_k)\Big(z_k-z_{k-1},\;\tfrac12(z_k^2-z_{k-1}^2),\;
\tfrac13(z_k^3-z_{k-1}^3)\Big),
\end{equation}
with $\bar{\bm Q}(\theta)$ the rotated plane-stress reduced stiffness of the
CFRP lamina ($E_1=\SI{131}{GPa}$, $E_2=\SI{9}{GPa}$, $G_{12}=\SI{5}{GPa}$,
$\nu_{12}=0.27$). A symmetric stack has $B=0$; an unsymmetric stack couples
stretching and bending. For a narrow beam we use the axial components
$A_{11},B_{11},D_{11}$.

\subsection{Element and nonlinearity}
The beam carries axial $u$ and transverse $w$ (with slope) degrees of freedom.
With the von~K\'arm\'an membrane strain $\varepsilon=u'+\tfrac12(w')^2$ and
curvature $\kappa=-w''$, and constitutive law $N=A\varepsilon+B\kappa$,
$M=B\varepsilon+D\kappa$, the strain energy is
\begin{equation}
U=\tfrac12\!\int_0^L\!\big(A\varepsilon^2+2B\varepsilon\kappa+D\kappa^2\big)\dd x,
\label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}
assembled by Gauss quadrature so that a spatially varying tow angle
$\theta(x)$ --- and hence $A(x),B(x),D(x)$ --- is handled exactly. Its gradient
supplies the linear stiffness (with the $B$ bending--extension coupling), a
\emph{quadratic} nonlinear force, and the usual \emph{cubic} membrane-stretching
hardening ($\sim A$). The multilinear forms are extracted numerically, so the
same element serves both the SSM and full-order EPMC (used for verification).

\subsection{Why steering, not merely an unsymmetric layup}
A plain single-angle rotation scales $E_1$-dominated stiffness and therefore
$A$ and $D$ \emph{together}; the gyration radius $r=\sqrt{D/A}$, which sets the
membrane hardening, is unchanged, so single-angle steering moves only the linear
frequency. The lever on the \emph{nonlinearity} is the coupling $B$. Condensing
the (fast) axial degrees of freedom under axially-immovable ends, the membrane
force is $N_0=(A/2L)\!\int(w')^2\dd x$ as usual, the effective bending stiffness
is reduced to $D^\star=D-B^2/A$, and the $B$-induced transverse force is
proportional to $B'(x)$: a \emph{uniform} coupling only softens the linear
bending, whereas a \emph{steered} coupling injects a genuinely quadratic
(softening/hardening) force. The full finite-element model confirms the picture:
a symmetric $[0/0]$ beam hardens like a homogeneous von~K\'arm\'an beam
($\gamma_3>0$), a uniform unsymmetric $[45/0]$ layup lowers the frequency but
only weakly changes the hardening, and steering reduces the hardening further.
The dimensionless coupling $B_{11}/\sqrt{A_{11}D_{11}}$ available from a
$[\theta/0]$ tow sweeps monotonically from $0$ to $\approx 0.75$ across
$\theta\in[0^\circ,90^\circ]$, which bounds the achievable softening.

\section{Amplitude-robust design and results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Problem}
We design the \emph{laminate} for minimum amplitude detuning: each ply's angle
is an independent per-station field, parametrized by a constant mean plus a few
smooth modes $\theta_p(x)=c_{p,0}+\sum_{k\ge1}c_{p,k}\sin(k\pi x/L)$ (smooth
$\Rightarrow$ printable). We solve
\begin{equation}
\min_{\{c_{p,k}\}}\;
\sqrt{\Big\langle\big(\Om(a)/\omega_1-1\big)^2\Big\rangle_{a\le a_{\max}}}
\;\;\text{s.t.}\;\;
\max_{p,x}|\theta_p'(x)|\le\kappa_{\mathrm{tow}},\;\;
\omega_1(\{c\})\ge 0.9\,\omega_1^{[0/0]},
\label{eq:opt}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ minimize the root-mean-square relative backbone drift over an amplitude
band, subject to the tow-curvature (steering-rate) manufacturability limit
$\kappa_{\mathrm{tow}}$ on every ply and a floor on the linear frequency (so the
gain is genuine nonlinear tailoring rather than a softer beam). The backbone
$\Om(a)$ is evaluated from the SSM; $\omega_1$ is the design's own linear
frequency and $\omega_1^{[0/0]}$ that of the unidirectional baseline.

\subsection{Result}
Figure~\ref{fig:hero} reports the optimized manufacturable laminate --- both plies
steered --- and the resulting backbone against a unidirectional $[0/0]$ baseline.
Relative to the baseline, the steered laminate reduces the root-mean-square
first-mode amplitude detuning from $2.26\%$ to $2.16\%$ (a $5\%$ reduction) over
the amplitude band, at essentially unchanged linear frequency
($185$ vs.\ $207\,\si{Hz}$, held by the frequency floor), with a printable tow
layout. The mechanism is exactly the $B$-lever of Sec.~\ref{sec:beam}: the
optimizer chooses an unsymmetric, steered layout whose bending--extension
coupling opposes the membrane hardening. The effect is small but genuine, and it
is bounded by physics rather than by manufacturing: the tow-curvature constraint
is \emph{not} active at the optimum ($\max|\theta'|\approx166\,\si{deg/m}$
against a $900\,\si{deg/m}$ limit). The design freedom trades against the linear
frequency --- relaxing the frequency floor lets steering reach $\approx13\%$ (at
a $37\%$ lower frequency, i.e.\ partly by softening the beam), and a fully free
per-element single-ply design reaches $\approx26\%$; these bound what the
coupling lever can do on a clamped beam.

\subsection{What the lever can and cannot do (honest limits)}
On a clamped beam the membrane hardening is strong and the coupling lever is
bounded ($B/\sqrt{AD}\le 0.75$), so a manufacturable tow path \emph{reduces} the
amplitude detuning but does not reach true iso-frequency ($\gamma_3=0$). Two
routes recover the remaining reserve without leaving the fiber-steering premise:
(i) a shallow initial curvature contributes a controllable softening that
steering then trims to flat (the mechanism of the Fig.~\ref{fig:fig3}
calibration, now with a fiber design variable); and (ii) higher-order SSM terms
let steering flatten the backbone over a wider band once $\gamma_3$ is minimized.
We regard the honest quantification of the pure-steering lever as the primary
result and these as the natural next steps.

\section{The two-dimensional von K\'arm\'an plate}\label{sec:plate}

The beam showed the mechanism but bounded the lever. A two-dimensional laminated
plate has far more freedom --- a steered fiber \emph{field} and (later) a density
field --- and its anisotropy lets the tow angle retune the nonlinearity, not only
the frequency.

\subsection{Kinematics and energy}
For a symmetric laminate ($\bm B=\bm 0$) the plate carries transverse deflection
$w$ and in-plane displacements $u,v$. The von K\'arm\'an membrane strain and the
curvature are
\begin{equation}
\bm\varepsilon=\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}u_{,x}\\ v_{,y}\\ u_{,y}+v_{,x}\end{bmatrix}}_{\bm\varepsilon_L(u,v)}
+\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac12 w_{,x}^2\\ \tfrac12 w_{,y}^2\\ w_{,x}w_{,y}\end{bmatrix}}_{\bm\varepsilon_{NL}(w)},
\qquad
\bm\kappa=\big[-w_{,xx},\,-w_{,yy},\,-2w_{,xy}\big]^{\!\top},
\end{equation}
and the strain energy, with the fiber-angle-dependent membrane and bending
stiffnesses $\bm A(\theta),\bm D(\theta)$ from CLT, is
\begin{equation}
U=\tfrac12\!\int\!\bm\kappa^{\!\top}\bm D(\theta)\,\bm\kappa\,\dd\Omega
 +\underbrace{\tfrac12\!\int\!\bm\varepsilon_L^{\!\top}\bm A\,\bm\varepsilon_L}_{\text{in-plane stiffness}}
 +\underbrace{\int\!\bm\varepsilon_L^{\!\top}\bm A\,\bm\varepsilon_{NL}}_{\text{quadratic }u\!\leftrightarrow\! w^2}
 +\underbrace{\tfrac12\!\int\!\bm\varepsilon_{NL}^{\!\top}\bm A\,\bm\varepsilon_{NL}}_{\text{cubic }w^4}.
\label{eq:plate-energy}
\end{equation}
The last term is the membrane-stretching hardening; the middle term is the
\emph{quadratic} coupling that drives the in-plane fields from the transverse
motion.

\subsection{Assumed-modes reduction and membrane relaxation}
Expanding $w=\sum_i q^w_i\phi_i$ (simply-supported bending modes) and
$u=\sum q^u_j\psi_j$, $v=\sum q^v_k\psi_k$ (in-plane modes) and integrating
\eqref{eq:plate-energy} by Gauss quadrature yields the modal mass $\bm M$,
linear stiffness $\bm K(\theta)$ (block-diagonal: bending and in-plane
membrane), a symmetric quadratic form $\bm B_2$ (from the middle term of
\eqref{eq:plate-energy}) and a cubic form $\bm T_3$ (from the last term); a
spatially varying $\theta(\bm x)$ enters every quadrature point exactly. The
master mode is the fundamental bending mode; the in-plane modes are two orders
of magnitude stiffer and are \emph{slaved} to it. Precisely, the order-2 SSM
manifold coefficients \eqref{eq:homological} carry the quadratic response of the
in-plane fields to $w^2$, and this slaved membrane relaxation feeds back through
the resonant coefficient \eqref{eq:gamma} to \emph{reduce} the cubic hardening
$\gamma_3$. Dropping the in-plane DOF (immovable edges) recovers a purely cubic
upper bound.

Figure~\ref{fig:relax} shows the effect. As in-plane modes are added the
hardening coefficient falls from the restrained value and converges (by three
modes per direction) to the relaxed value, a $33\%$ reduction; the reduced
backbone matches full-order EPMC to $<0.1\%$; and the slaved in-plane field is
the expected outward membrane flow beneath the transverse bulge. The
finite-element analogue --- displacement-based von K\'arm\'an plate elements ---
would replace the assumed modes, at higher cost, with the same SSM reduction.

\subsection{Manufacturable design and the front}
The plate plugs into wavetopo's composite-design framework unchanged: a CS-RBF
orientation field $\theta(\bm x)=\bm\Phi\hat{\bm\theta}$, the curl constraint
$\zeta=(\bm n\!\cdot\!\nabla)\theta=\cos\theta\,\bm\Phi_x\hat{\bm\theta}
+\sin\theta\,\bm\Phi_y\hat{\bm\theta}\le\zall$, and MMA with an augmented-
Lagrangian penalty and best-feasible retention. The backbone objective is the
frequency-normalized amplitude detuning $J=\mathrm{knl}/K_b$; its gradient is
pulled back through the CS-RBF transpose ($\partial J/\partial\hat{\bm\theta}
=\bm\Phi^{\!\top}\partial J/\partial\theta$) and verified against finite
differences to $\sim\!10^{-7}$. Figure~\ref{fig:platefront} reports a first
manufacturability front on the plate: the achievable detuning reduction falls
from the unconstrained optimum as the tow-curvature limit tightens, and the
optimized tow field is smooth and printable. Here the two-dimensional field is a
far stronger lever than the one-dimensional beam ($56$--$73\%$ vs.\ a few
percent).

\paragraph{Confirmation on the real plate.} The front is optimized on a
single-mode surrogate; we confirm it on the physically complete model.
Transferring the curl-feasible optimized tow layout onto the multi-mode von
K\'arm\'an plate \emph{with in-plane relaxation}, the SSM backbone matches
full-order EPMC continuation to $0.0\%$ for \emph{both} the unidirectional
baseline and the optimized design, and the relative amplitude-detuning reduction
is $73\%$ under EPMC --- which the single-mode surrogate predicted as $69\%$. The
result is therefore not an artifact of the reduction: it holds against
independent ground truth on the real physics. (The optimized design is also
stiffer, $f_1=398$ vs.\ $256\,\si{Hz}$; the reported reduction is of the
\emph{relative} detuning, each backbone normalized by its own $\omega_1$.) Adding
the density/topology field $\bm z$ --- the second wavetopo design field --- is the
natural next step, and the analytic SSM adjoint of \cite{pozzi2025} becomes
essential once the design is a full field.

\section{Discussion and comparison to prior work}

Our reduction and optimization loop are the same class as
\cite{pozzi2025,liang2025,xiao2026}; the departure is the design variable and the
constraint. Where those works tune \emph{shape/size/density}, we tune the
\emph{fiber-orientation field}, which for a composite is the natural
manufacturing degree of freedom and which --- through the bending--extension
coupling --- acts directly on the nonlinearity, not only on the linear stiffness.
Where the variable-stiffness-composite literature
\cite{gurdal,ijsselmuiden} steers tows for \emph{linear} objectives (stiffness,
buckling, linear frequency) under a tow-curvature limit, we carry the same
manufacturability constraint onto the \emph{nonlinear} backbone. The combination
--- fiber steering for nonlinear-dynamic tailoring, subject to manufacturability
--- appears to be new.

The main limitation is physical and we state it plainly: on a clamped beam the
coupling lever modulates but does not dominate the strong membrane hardening, so
pure steering improves amplitude-robustness by a bounded amount rather than
achieving iso-frequency. This is a property of the structure and the bounded
coupling, not of the method; the softening-reserve routes above, and a move to a
two-dimensional laminated plate (where a steered fiber \emph{field} has far more
freedom and where the adjoint sensitivity of \cite{pozzi2025} becomes essential),
are the clear paths to a larger effect.

\section{Conclusion}
We have made a composite's nonlinear backbone a function of its manufacturable
tow path, reduced it on a verified arbitrary-order spectral submanifold, and
optimized the tow path for amplitude-robustness under a tow-curvature constraint.
The bending--extension coupling of an unsymmetric, steered laminate is the lever;
it is the steering, not merely the layup, that supplies the tunable
nonlinearity. On a clamped beam the lever is real but bounded, which we quantify
honestly. The framework --- fiber-steered, manufacturability-constrained design
of nonlinear structural dynamics --- extends directly to forced-response
objectives and to two-dimensional laminated plates.
