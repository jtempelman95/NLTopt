\begin{abstract}
Reduced-order models on spectral submanifolds (SSMs) have recently made it
practical to \emph{optimize} the nonlinear dynamic response of structures --- the
backbone and forced-response curves --- because both the response and its
sensitivity are available analytically. To date that machinery has been driven
by \emph{shape}, \emph{size}, and \emph{density}-topology design variables. We
introduce a different, and for composites more natural, design variable: the
\emph{fiber-orientation field} (tow path) $\theta(x)$, and we impose the
\emph{tow-curvature manufacturability constraint} that governs automated fiber
placement. We build a laminated von~K\'arm\'an beam whose per-station laminate
stiffnesses $A(\theta),B(\theta),D(\theta)$ follow from classical laminate
theory, reduce its first mode on an arbitrary-order 2-D SSM, and optimize the
tow path for an \emph{amplitude-robust} (minimally detuning) resonance. The
enabling physics is the laminate bending--extension coupling $B$: a plain
single-angle rotation scales $EI$ and $EA$ together and cannot touch the
hardening, whereas an unsymmetric, \emph{steered} layup injects a tunable
quadratic force that partially cancels the cubic membrane hardening. We verify
the reduction against the analytic Duffing backbone (including the $-21/256$
fifth-order coefficient) and against full-order harmonic-balance/EPMC
continuation, and we reproduce two figures of a recent SSM-optimization paper as
calibration. On a clamped beam the membrane hardening dominates and the coupling
lever is bounded, so at fixed linear frequency a manufacturable tow layout
\emph{reduces} the amplitude detuning only modestly (a few percent) --- a limit
set by the coupling lever, not by manufacturability. We report this honestly and
identify the routes along which the effect grows. On a two-dimensional laminated
plate the lever is far stronger: there the layup is symmetric, so the coupling
$B$ is gone and the anisotropic membrane stiffness itself becomes the knob ---
the linear frequency (set by the bending functional) and the hardening (set by
the relaxed membrane functional) sample the same tow field through different
operators, so a steered field can hold one while cutting the other. Driving the
design by the plate's own SSM backbone coefficient --- reproduced in closed form
by static in-plane condensation and differentiated by an analytic adjoint --- a
curl-feasible steered tow field reduces the relative amplitude detuning by $50\%$
at held linear frequency, a figure the SSM backbone, full-order EPMC, and
brute-force up/down frequency sweeps all confirm. The tangible consequence is a
resonator that stays linear to larger amplitude: the steering \emph{removes the
jump}, widening the panel's jump- and hysteresis-free operating range by
$1.42\times$ (and further with an added topology field), the property a resonant
sensor or harvester actually needs. The same adjoint, applied to the nonsymmetric
aeroelastic (piston-theory) operator on a \emph{conforming} finite element (needed once
bend--twist coupling twists the mode shapes --- a point at which a global-mode Ritz model
fails), recovers a \emph{supersonic panel-flutter} boundary by $9\times$ at held
frequency: off-axis fibers crater a uniform panel's flutter, and a steered field places
the same coupling to recover it. To our knowledge this is the first design of a
composite's \emph{nonlinear} backbone through a manufacturable fiber-orientation
field, and the verified framework is the contribution.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Nonlinearity in the design phase is increasingly functional rather than
parasitic: frequency division, vibration mitigation, energy harvesting, and
MEMS resonators all exploit amplitude-dependent stiffness
\cite{shaw,touze}. Tailoring that behavior means shaping the
\emph{backbone curve} --- the frequency--amplitude relation of a nonlinear
normal mode. The backbone is classically traced by numerical continuation of
the harmonic-balance or shooting equations, but reduction onto a
\emph{spectral submanifold} (SSM) \cite{haller2016,jain2022} yields the backbone
and, crucially, its parameter sensitivity in closed form, at a cost independent
of the number of design variables when the adjoint method is used.

Three recent works establish SSM/ROM-based optimization of nonlinear dynamics:
Pozzi \emph{et al.}~\cite{pozzi2025} derive adjoint sensitivities of the
SSM backbone and shape a von~K\'arm\'an beam to a prescribed softening target;
Liang \emph{et al.}~\cite{liang2025} optimize nonlinear forced-response curves
(peak amplitude, hardening/softening, saddle-node spacing) via SSM reduction;
and Xiao's thesis~\cite{xiao2026} develops ROM-based optimization of nonlinear
systems broadly. All three parametrize the design by \emph{shape, size, or
element density}. None uses a fiber-orientation field, and none imposes a
manufacturability constraint.

Separately, \emph{variable-stiffness composites} --- laminates whose fiber tows
are steered along curvilinear paths by automated fiber placement --- are a mature
design space, but the objectives pursued there are almost entirely
\emph{linear}: in-plane stiffness, buckling load, and linear natural
frequencies \cite{gurdal,ijsselmuiden}, subject to a tow-curvature (steering
radius) manufacturability limit. The intersection --- steering the tow path to
tailor a composite's \emph{nonlinear} backbone, under manufacturability --- is,
to our knowledge, open. That intersection is the subject of this paper.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item An arbitrary-order 2-D SSM reduction (Sec.~\ref{sec:ssm}) built directly
from a finite-element mechanical system, verified against the analytic Duffing
backbone and against full-order EPMC continuation, and calibrated by reproducing
two figures of \cite{pozzi2025}.
\item A laminated von~K\'arm\'an beam (Sec.~\ref{sec:beam}) whose nonlinear
backbone is a function of the fiber-orientation field, exposing the
bending--extension coupling $B(\theta)$ as the design lever, together with the
observation that it is the fiber \emph{steering} (the spatial gradient of
$\theta$), not merely an unsymmetric layup, that supplies the tunable quadratic
nonlinearity once the axial degrees of freedom are slaved.
\item A manufacturability-constrained optimization of the tow path for an
amplitude-robust resonance (Sec.~\ref{sec:results}), with an honest account of
what the coupling lever can and cannot achieve on a clamped beam.
\item A two-dimensional laminated von~K\'arm\'an plate (Sec.~\ref{sec:plate})
whose symmetric layup removes the coupling lever and exposes the anisotropic
membrane stiffness itself as the knob: the linear frequency and the hardening
sample the same tow field through different operators, so a steered field holds
one while cutting the other. We drive the design by the plate's own SSM backbone
coefficient --- reproduced in closed form by static in-plane condensation and
differentiated by an analytic adjoint (verified to $10^{-8}$) --- for a $50\%$
detuning reduction at held frequency, confirmed by EPMC and by brute-force
up/down frequency sweeps that trace the SSM-predicted damped forced response.
\item The second manufacturable field --- structural \emph{topology}
(Sec.~\ref{sec:topopt}) --- coupled to the same SSM backbone objective: a
three-field density with SIMP, filter and Heaviside projection, whose density
sensitivity is analytic and FD-verified and reuses the same adjoint solve as the
orientation gradient, optimized jointly with the tow angle under curl, volume and
frequency-band constraints to a connected, symmetric topology that voids the
membrane-stretch antinodes.
\item A concrete account of the \emph{engineering utility}
(Sec.~\ref{sec:utility}): the halved hardening widens the panel's jump- and
hysteresis-free operating range by $1.42\times$ ($\sim\!2.4\times$ with the added
topology field), a property that maps directly onto the requirements of resonant
sensors, vibration energy harvesters, and hysteresis-free actuators/MEMS --- and
that is reached by reshaping the \emph{nonlinear} response at fixed linear frequency
and fixed mass, a lever no classical composite knob provides.
\item Designing an \emph{instability boundary}, not only a backbone
(Sec.~\ref{sec:flutter}): the same left-eigenvector adjoint, applied to the nonsymmetric
aeroelastic (piston-theory) pencil on a \emph{conforming} FE, recovers a supersonic
panel-flutter boundary by $9\times$ at held frequency and mass (FD-verified $2.1\times10^{-7}$,
calibrated to $\lambda_{\mathrm{cr}}=343$) --- off-axis fibers' bend--twist coupling craters a
uniform panel's flutter and steering places it to recover. En route, an FE cross-check
overturned a plausible global-mode result (a spurious $2\times$), a cautionary point in its own
right; a supercritical Stuart--Landau coefficient shows the post-flutter limit cycle is benign.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The nonlinear system and its spectral submanifold}\label{sec:ssm}

\subsection{Second-order form}
We consider a finite-element mechanical system
\begin{equation}
\bm{M}\ddot{\bm{x}} + \bm{C}\dot{\bm{x}} + \bm{K}\bm{x}
 + \bm{f}_{\mathrm{nl}}(\bm{x}) = \bm 0,
\qquad
\bm f_{\mathrm{nl}}(\bm x) = \bm B_2(\bm x,\bm x) + \bm T_3(\bm x,\bm x,\bm x),
\label{eq:eom}
\end{equation}
with symmetric mass, damping and stiffness matrices and displacement-dependent
nonlinearity split into its symmetric quadratic ($\bm B_2$) and cubic ($\bm T_3$)
multilinear forms. In first-order state space $\bm z=[\bm x;\dot{\bm x}]$,
\begin{equation}
\bm{\mathcal B}\dot{\bm z}=\bm{\mathcal A}\bm z+\bm F(\bm z),\quad
\bm{\mathcal B}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm C&\bm M\\\bm M&\bm 0\end{bmatrix},\;
\bm{\mathcal A}=\begin{bmatrix}-\bm K&\bm 0\\\bm 0&\bm M\end{bmatrix},\;
\bm F=\begin{bmatrix}-\bm f_{\mathrm{nl}}(\bm x)\\\bm 0\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:firstorder}
\end{equation}
The master mode $(\omega_0,\bm\varphi)$ solves $(\bm K-\omega_0^2\bm M)\bm\varphi=\bm0$
with $\bm\varphi^{\!\top}\bm M\bm\varphi=1$; its state-space eigenvalue is
$\lambda_1=-\zeta_0\omega_0+i\omega_0\sqrt{1-\zeta_0^2}$ with
$\zeta_0=\bm\varphi^{\!\top}\bm C\bm\varphi/(2\omega_0)$.

\subsection{Invariance equation}
A spectral submanifold $\mathcal W$ is the smoothest invariant manifold of
\eqref{eq:firstorder} tangent, at the origin, to a chosen spectral subspace $E$
of the linearization --- here the two-dimensional eigenspace of the master pair
$\lambda_1,\lambda_2=\bar\lambda_1$ \cite{haller2016,jain2022}. It carries a
single nonlinear mode: trajectories launched on $\mathcal W$ remain on it, so the
system's vector field is everywhere \emph{tangent} to $\mathcal W$. We use this
tangency to compute $\mathcal W$ and the dynamics it carries.

Parametrize $\mathcal W$ by a two-dimensional chart $\bm p=(p_1,p_2)\in\C^2$
through an embedding $\bm W:\C^2\!\to\C^{2N}$ and let the induced (reduced)
dynamics on the chart be $\bm R$:
\begin{equation}
\bm z=\bm W(\bm p),\qquad \dot{\bm p}=\bm R(\bm p).
\label{eq:param}
\end{equation}
Differentiating $\bm z=\bm W(\bm p)$ in time gives
$\dot{\bm z}=\mathrm D\bm W(\bm p)\,\dot{\bm p}=\mathrm D\bm W(\bm p)\,\bm R(\bm p)$;
requiring this to equal the true velocity field~\eqref{eq:firstorder} on the
manifold yields the \textbf{invariance equation}
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;
\bm{\mathcal B}\,\mathrm D\bm W(\bm p)\,\bm R(\bm p)
=\bm{\mathcal A}\,\bm W(\bm p)+\bm F\!\big(\bm W(\bm p)\big)
\;}
\label{eq:invariance}
\end{equation}
a single functional equation in the two unknowns $\bm W$ (the geometry) and
$\bm R$ (the reduced dynamics). Because $E$ is spanned by the master
eigenvectors, we take conjugate coordinates $p_2=\bar p_1$; a real physical
motion is then $\bm x=[\bm I\;\bm0]\bm W(\bm p)$ evaluated on $p_2=\bar p_1$.

\subsection{Existence, uniqueness, and what makes the reduction exact}
The invariance equation~\eqref{eq:invariance} has many formal invariant-manifold
solutions; what singles out \emph{the} SSM is a spectral gap. Write the eigenvalues
of the linearized pencil $(\bm{\mathcal A},\bm{\mathcal B})$ as
$\{\lambda_j,\bar\lambda_j\}_{j=1}^{N}$. The master pair $\lambda_1,\bar\lambda_1$ spans
$E$; call the rest the \emph{outer} spectrum. Haller and Ponsioen \cite{haller2016}
show that, provided the outer eigenvalues are strictly further from the imaginary axis
than the master pair (the \emph{spectral quotient} condition) and provided no outer
eigenvalue is an integer combination $\lambda_o=m_1\lambda_1+m_2\bar\lambda_1$ of the
master pair (a \emph{non-resonance} condition, up to the truncation order), there is a
\emph{unique smoothest} invariant manifold tangent to $E$ at the origin --- the
spectral submanifold. Uniqueness is what makes the reduction principled: the backbone
we compute is not one of many possible reduced models but the exact restriction of the
full dynamics to its slowest two-dimensional invariant surface.

The two conditions also explain the method's failure modes, which we meet in practice.
The non-resonance condition can fail at a specific order when
$m_1\lambda_1+m_2\bar\lambda_1$ approaches an outer eigenvalue --- an \emph{internal
resonance} (e.g.\ a $3{:}1$ ratio $3\omega_1\!\approx\!\omega_2$); then the
corresponding homological operator is (near-)singular, the coefficient at that order
blows up, and the two-dimensional SSM must be enlarged to include the resonant mode.
The spectral-gap condition is what bounds the amplitude range: the manifold is
guaranteed near the origin, and the polynomial parametrization below is its Taylor
expansion there, so it is accurate within a radius that shrinks as the nonlinearity
strengthens (Sec.~\ref{sec:handoff}). We use the \emph{normal-form} style of the
parametrization method \cite{jain2022}: rather than force the reduced dynamics to be
linear (which would push all nonlinearity into the geometry $\bm W$ and demand the
strong non-resonance conditions), we keep the near-resonant terms in $\bm R$ --- these
are precisely the backbone coefficients $\gamma_{2k+1}$ --- and annihilate only the
non-resonant part, which is always invertible under light damping.

\subsection{Solution by a graded power series}
Expand~\eqref{eq:invariance} as power series in $\bm p$,
\begin{equation}
\bm W(\bm p)=\!\!\sum_{|\bm m|\ge1}\!\!\bm w_{\bm m}\bm p^{\bm m},\qquad
\bm R(\bm p)=\!\!\sum_{|\bm m|\ge1}\!\!\bm R_{\bm m}\bm p^{\bm m},\qquad
\bm p^{\bm m}=p_1^{m_1}p_2^{m_2},
\end{equation}
with multi-index $\bm m=(m_1,m_2)$. At first order tangency to $E$ fixes
$\bm w_{\bm e_1}=\bm v_1,\ \bm w_{\bm e_2}=\bm v_2$ (the master right
eigenvectors) and a diagonal linear reduced flow $R^1_{\bm e_1}=\lambda_1,\
R^2_{\bm e_2}=\lambda_2$. Collecting~\eqref{eq:invariance} at a general order
$\bm m$ ($|\bm m|\ge2$) separates the unknown coefficient of that order and gives
the \textbf{homological equation}
\begin{equation}
(\Lambda_{\bm m}\bm{\mathcal B}-\bm{\mathcal A})\,\bm w_{\bm m}
+\bm{\mathcal B}\big(\bm v_1 R^1_{\bm m}+\bm v_2 R^2_{\bm m}\big)
=\bm G_{\bm m},\qquad \Lambda_{\bm m}=m_1\lambda_1+m_2\lambda_2 .
\label{eq:homological}
\end{equation}
The left side is the linearization of~\eqref{eq:invariance} about the master
subspace: the diagonal term $\Lambda_{\bm m}\bm w_{\bm m}$ comes from
$\bm{\mathcal B}\,\mathrm D\bm W\,\bm R$ paired with the linear flow, and the
$\bm v_j R^j_{\bm m}$ term is the contribution of the unknown reduced coefficient
at this order. The right side $\bm G_{\bm m}$ collects everything already known
from \emph{lower} orders: the order-$\bm m$ part of the nonlinear force
$-[\bm F(\bm W)]_{\bm m}$, assembled by the multi-index convolutions
\begin{equation}
[\bm B_2(\bm x,\bm x)]_{\bm m}\!=\!\!\sum_{\bm a+\bm b=\bm m}\!\!
\bm B_2(\bm w^{x}_{\bm a},\bm w^{x}_{\bm b}),\quad
[\bm T_3(\bm x,\bm x,\bm x)]_{\bm m}\!=\!\!\!\sum_{\bm a+\bm b+\bm c=\bm m}\!\!\!
\bm T_3(\bm w^{x}_{\bm a},\bm w^{x}_{\bm b},\bm w^{x}_{\bm c}),
\end{equation}
($\bm w^{x}$ the displacement block of $\bm w$), minus the cross-terms
$\bm{\mathcal B}\sum a_j\,\bm w_{\bm a}R^j_{\bm b}$ generated by
$\mathrm D\bm W\,\bm R$ from lower-order manifold and reduced coefficients. Thus
\eqref{eq:homological} is solved recursively, order by order.

\subsection{Resonances, the reduced dynamics, and the backbone}
The operator $\Lambda_{\bm m}\bm{\mathcal B}-\bm{\mathcal A}$ is singular exactly
when $\Lambda_{\bm m}$ hits an eigenvalue of the pencil $(\bm{\mathcal A},
\bm{\mathcal B})$ --- an (inner) resonance. For the master pair this occurs at the
indices $\bm m=(k{+}1,k)$, where $\Lambda_{(k+1,k)}=\lambda_1$ in the
conservative case (and lies near it under light damping). At these indices one
cannot solve for $\bm w_{\bm m}$ alone; the (near-)singular component is instead
carried by the reduced dynamics. Projecting~\eqref{eq:homological} onto the left
eigenvector $\bm u_1$ (defined by $\bm u_1^{*}\bm{\mathcal A}=\lambda_1
\bm u_1^{*}\bm{\mathcal B}$ and normalized $\bm u_1^{*}\bm{\mathcal B}\bm v_1=1$)
annihilates the singular operator and isolates the reduced coefficient
\begin{equation}
\gamma_{2k+1}\;:=\;R^1_{(k+1,k)}\;=\;\bm u_1^{*}\,\bm G_{(k+1,k)} .
\label{eq:gamma}
\end{equation}
The remaining (now solvable) system fixes $\bm w_{\bm m}$ under the normal-form
gauge $\bm u_1^{*}\bm{\mathcal B}\bm w_{\bm m}=0$, which --- as one verifies by
applying $\bm u_1^{*}$ to the residual --- is met automatically by the solve,
damped or not. Non-resonant indices set $\bm R_{\bm m}=\bm 0$ and invert
$\Lambda_{\bm m}\bm{\mathcal B}-\bm{\mathcal A}$ directly. Equation~\eqref{eq:gamma}
is exactly an \emph{adjoint} projection --- $\bm u_1$ is the adjoint mode --- which
is why the same construction later delivers inexpensive design sensitivities.

Keeping only the resonant terms, the reduced dynamics is
$\dot p_1=\big(\lambda_1+\sum_{k\ge1}\gamma_{2k+1}\,p_1^{k+1}p_2^{k}\big)$. On the
conjugate orbit $p_1=\tfrac{\rho}{2}e^{i\theta},\ p_2=\bar p_1$ one has
$p_1^{k+1}p_2^{k}=|p_1|^{2k}p_1=(\rho/2)^{2k}p_1$, so
$\dot p_1/p_1=\lambda_1+\sum_k\gamma_{2k+1}(\rho/2)^{2k}$ and the \textbf{backbone}
(instantaneous frequency) and modal decay are
\begin{equation}
\Om(\rho)=\Imag\!\Big(\lambda_1+\!\sum_{k\ge1}\gamma_{2k+1}(\rho/2)^{2k}\Big),
\qquad
\alpha(\rho)=\Real\!\Big(\lambda_1+\!\sum_{k\ge1}\gamma_{2k+1}(\rho/2)^{2k}\Big).
\label{eq:backbone}
\end{equation}
The cubic coefficient $\gamma_3$ alone yields a monotone parabola --- hardening if
$\Imag\gamma_3>0$, softening if $<0$; the quintic $\gamma_5$ (order-5 reduction) is
what lets a softening branch turn around and harden. Physical amplitudes on the
backbone follow by sampling $\bm x=[\bm I\;\bm0]\bm W(\bm p)$ over one period.

Figure~\ref{fig:manifold} makes the object concrete for the plate: evaluating
$\bm z=\bm W(\bm p)$ on the reduced disk draws the two-dimensional submanifold as a
graph over the master mode's phase plane. It is tangent, at the origin, to the flat
linear eigenplane, and it \emph{curves} away from it exactly through the slaved
coordinates --- here the in-plane (membrane) motion, which is driven at twice the
transverse frequency and grows like the square of the transverse amplitude. That
curvature \emph{is} the membrane relaxation of Sec.~\ref{sec:plate}; the backbone is
the family of periodic orbits riding on the surface. The whole nonlinear response is
thus a smooth function of \emph{two} coordinates, which is why the reduced backbone
and its design gradient cost the same whether the model has ten degrees of freedom or
ten thousand --- and why the objective landscape it presents to the optimizer is
analytic (\S\ref{sec:prosncons}).

\paragraph{Consistency and verification.}
The multilinear forms $\bm B_2,\bm T_3$ are extracted from the finite-element
nonlinearity by even/odd decomposition and polarization, so the SSM and a
harmonic-balance model consume \emph{identical} nonlinearities. Table~\ref{tab:verify}
summarizes the verification. For the single-degree-of-freedom Duffing oscillator
$\ddot x+\omega_0^2x+\varepsilon x^3=0$ the reduction reproduces the exact
first-order coefficient and, at fifth order, the analytic fourth-order backbone
coefficient
$\Om/\omega_0=1+\tfrac38\varepsilon A^2-\tfrac{21}{256}\varepsilon^2A^4+\dots$
to within a few percent. Against full-order EPMC continuation of a
quadratically-coupled two-degree-of-freedom system and of a clamped--clamped
von~K\'arm\'an beam, the backbones agree to sub-percent at the amplitudes where
the expansion is converged.

\subsection{Backbone sensitivity and calibration against prior work}
For an objective $J$ built on the SSM the only design-dependence is through the reduced
coefficients --- the master frequency $\omega_1(\theta)$ and the backbone coefficients
$\gamma_{2k+1}(\theta)$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gamma} --- and two sensitivities suffice. The
frequency is a symmetric Rayleigh quotient, so $\partial\omega_1^2/\partial\theta
=\bm\varphi^{\!\top}(\partial\bm K/\partial\theta)\bm\varphi$ by Fox--Kapoor, with no
eigenvector derivative. Each $\gamma_{2k+1}=\bm u_1^{*}\bm G_m$ is a multilinear
contraction of the master eigenvectors and the $\theta$-dependent operators
$\bm M,\bm K,\bm B_2,\bm T_3$, whose derivatives all reduce to a single laminate rotation
$\partial\bar{\bm Q}/\partial\theta$; the eigenvector-derivative terms it also carries
collapse, by the left-eigenvector (adjoint) structure of the projection, to \emph{one}
linear solve reused across \emph{every} design degree of freedom. The design sensitivity
therefore costs one reduced solve regardless of the number of tow-path variables --- the
property that makes a \emph{field} of orientations tractable. We derive this adjoint in
closed form for the relaxed cubic (Eq.~\eqref{eq:gammaeffadj}), for the coupled
density--orientation problem (Sec.~\ref{sec:topopt}), and for the flutter boundary
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:flutter-adj}), each verified against finite differences to
$\lesssim\!10^{-7}$. As a first small-parameter calibration we instead finite-difference
the cheap backbone and reproduce two results of \cite{pozzi2025,li2024}. First
(their Fig.~2, Fig.~\ref{fig:fig2} here), the backbone sensitivity of two coupled
oscillators predicts the perturbed backbone, $\Om(\bm\mu+\delta\bm\mu)\approx
\Om(\bm\mu)+\partial_{\bm\mu}\Om\,\delta\bm\mu$, to $\sim\!10^{-5}$ over the
system's soften-then-harden ``backward-C'' backbone (which requires the
fifth-order SSM to capture). Second (their Fig.~3, Fig.~\ref{fig:fig3} here), we
shape an initially curved beam to a prescribed softening target and confirm the
optimized backbone against EPMC to $<0.1\%$ at both target points.
We note two honest departures in the second calibration: we use the
axially-condensed shallow-arch model rather than a full three-DOF-per-node
element, and we place the target amplitudes inside the cubic-SSM's verified
window (the paper's larger target sits beyond it, requiring higher order). We
also observe that Eq.~(33) of \cite{pozzi2025}, taken literally, places a
same-sign quadratic coupling on both masses, which is not energy-consistent; we
use the potential-derived spring, which leaves the reported eigenvalue unchanged.

\section{Fiber-steered laminated von K\'arm\'an beam}\label{sec:beam}

\subsection{Laminate stiffnesses from the tow angle}
For a laminate of plies at angles $\theta_k$ (relative to the beam axis) the
extensional, coupling and bending stiffnesses follow from classical laminate
theory,
\begin{equation}
(A,B,D)=\sum_k \bar{\bm Q}(\theta_k)\Big(z_k-z_{k-1},\;\tfrac12(z_k^2-z_{k-1}^2),\;
\tfrac13(z_k^3-z_{k-1}^3)\Big),
\end{equation}
with $\bar{\bm Q}(\theta)$ the rotated plane-stress reduced stiffness of the
CFRP lamina ($E_1=\SI{131}{GPa}$, $E_2=\SI{9}{GPa}$, $G_{12}=\SI{5}{GPa}$,
$\nu_{12}=0.27$). The on-axis stiffnesses $Q_{11}=E_1/(1-\nu_{12}\nu_{21})$,
$Q_{22}=E_2/(1-\nu_{12}\nu_{21})$, $Q_{12}=\nu_{12}Q_{22}$, $Q_{66}=G_{12}$
($\nu_{21}=\nu_{12}E_2/E_1$) rotate to the fiber angle in the invariant (Tsai--Pagano)
form
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\bar Q_{11}&=U_1+U_2\cos2\theta+U_3\cos4\theta, &\quad
\bar Q_{12}&=U_4-U_3\cos4\theta,\\
\bar Q_{22}&=U_1-U_2\cos2\theta+U_3\cos4\theta, &\quad
\bar Q_{66}&=U_5-U_3\cos4\theta,\\
\bar Q_{16}&=\tfrac12U_2\sin2\theta+U_3\sin4\theta, &\quad
\bar Q_{26}&=\tfrac12U_2\sin2\theta-U_3\sin4\theta,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:qbar}
\end{equation}
the $U_i$ fixed material invariants (combinations of $Q_{11},Q_{12},Q_{22},Q_{66}$). The
off-diagonal $\bar Q_{16},\bar Q_{26}$ --- the extension--shear / bend--twist couplings,
nonzero except at $\theta\in\{0,\tfrac{\pi}{2}\}$ --- are the anisotropy the steering
exploits, and the derivative that seeds every design gradient (Sec.~\ref{sec:ssm}) is the
elementary trigonometric $\partial\bar{\bm Q}/\partial\theta$ of~\eqref{eq:qbar}
(e.g.\ $\partial_\theta\bar Q_{16}=U_2\cos2\theta+4U_3\cos4\theta$). A symmetric stack has
$B=0$; an unsymmetric stack couples stretching and bending. For a narrow beam we use the
axial components $A_{11},B_{11},D_{11}$.

\subsection{Element and nonlinearity}
The beam carries axial $u$ and transverse $w$ (with slope) degrees of freedom.
With the von~K\'arm\'an membrane strain $\varepsilon=u'+\tfrac12(w')^2$ and
curvature $\kappa=-w''$, and constitutive law $N=A\varepsilon+B\kappa$,
$M=B\varepsilon+D\kappa$, the strain energy is
\begin{equation}
U=\tfrac12\!\int_0^L\!\big(A\varepsilon^2+2B\varepsilon\kappa+D\kappa^2\big)\dd x,
\label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}
assembled by Gauss quadrature so that a spatially varying tow angle
$\theta(x)$ --- and hence $A(x),B(x),D(x)$ --- is handled exactly. Its gradient
supplies the linear stiffness (with the $B$ bending--extension coupling), a
\emph{quadratic} nonlinear force, and the usual \emph{cubic} membrane-stretching
hardening ($\sim A$). The multilinear forms are extracted numerically, so the
same element serves both the SSM and full-order EPMC (used for verification).

\subsection{Why steering, not merely an unsymmetric layup}
A plain single-angle rotation scales $E_1$-dominated stiffness and therefore
$A$ and $D$ \emph{together}; the gyration radius $r=\sqrt{D/A}$, which sets the
membrane hardening, is unchanged, so single-angle steering moves only the linear
frequency. The lever on the \emph{nonlinearity} is the coupling $B$. Condensing
the (fast) axial degrees of freedom under axially-immovable ends, the membrane
force is $N_0=(A/2L)\!\int(w')^2\dd x$ as usual, the effective bending stiffness
is reduced to $D^\star=D-B^2/A$, and the $B$-induced transverse force is
proportional to $B'(x)$: a \emph{uniform} coupling only softens the linear
bending, whereas a \emph{steered} coupling injects a genuinely quadratic
(softening/hardening) force. The full finite-element model confirms the picture:
a symmetric $[0/0]$ beam hardens like a homogeneous von~K\'arm\'an beam
($\gamma_3>0$), a uniform unsymmetric $[45/0]$ layup lowers the frequency but
only weakly changes the hardening, and steering reduces the hardening further.
The dimensionless coupling $B_{11}/\sqrt{A_{11}D_{11}}$ available from a
$[\theta/0]$ tow sweeps monotonically from $0$ to $\approx 0.75$ across
$\theta\in[0^\circ,90^\circ]$, which bounds the achievable softening.

\section{Amplitude-robust design and results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Problem}
We design the \emph{laminate} for minimum amplitude detuning: each ply's angle
is an independent per-station field, parametrized by a constant mean plus a few
smooth modes $\theta_p(x)=c_{p,0}+\sum_{k\ge1}c_{p,k}\sin(k\pi x/L)$ (smooth
$\Rightarrow$ printable). We solve
\begin{equation}
\min_{\{c_{p,k}\}}\;
\sqrt{\Big\langle\big(\Om(a)/\omega_1-1\big)^2\Big\rangle_{a\le a_{\max}}}
\;\;\text{s.t.}\;\;
\max_{p,x}|\theta_p'(x)|\le\kappa_{\mathrm{tow}},\;\;
\omega_1(\{c\})\ge 0.9\,\omega_1^{[0/0]},
\label{eq:opt}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ minimize the root-mean-square relative backbone drift over an amplitude
band, subject to the tow-curvature (steering-rate) manufacturability limit
$\kappa_{\mathrm{tow}}$ on every ply and a floor on the linear frequency (so the
gain is genuine nonlinear tailoring rather than a softer beam). The backbone
$\Om(a)$ is evaluated from the SSM; $\omega_1$ is the design's own linear
frequency and $\omega_1^{[0/0]}$ that of the unidirectional baseline.

\subsection{Result}
Figure~\ref{fig:hero} reports the optimized manufacturable laminate --- both plies
steered --- and the resulting backbone against a unidirectional $[0/0]$ baseline.
Relative to the baseline, the steered laminate reduces the root-mean-square
first-mode amplitude detuning from $2.26\%$ to $2.16\%$ (a $5\%$ reduction) over
the amplitude band, at essentially unchanged linear frequency
($185$ vs.\ $207\,\si{Hz}$, held by the frequency floor), with a printable tow
layout. The mechanism is exactly the $B$-lever of Sec.~\ref{sec:beam}: the
optimizer chooses an unsymmetric, steered layout whose bending--extension
coupling opposes the membrane hardening. The effect is small but genuine, and it
is bounded by physics rather than by manufacturing: the tow-curvature constraint
is \emph{not} active at the optimum ($\max|\theta'|\approx166\,\si{deg/m}$
against a $900\,\si{deg/m}$ limit). The design freedom trades against the linear
frequency --- relaxing the frequency floor lets steering reach $\approx13\%$ (at
a $37\%$ lower frequency, i.e.\ partly by softening the beam), and a fully free
per-element single-ply design reaches $\approx26\%$; these bound what the
coupling lever can do on a clamped beam.

\subsection{What the lever can and cannot do (honest limits)}
On a clamped beam the membrane hardening is strong and the coupling lever is
bounded ($B/\sqrt{AD}\le 0.75$), so a manufacturable tow path \emph{reduces} the
amplitude detuning but does not reach true iso-frequency ($\gamma_3=0$). Two
routes recover the remaining reserve without leaving the fiber-steering premise:
(i) a shallow initial curvature contributes a controllable softening that
steering then trims to flat (the mechanism of the Fig.~\ref{fig:fig3}
calibration, now with a fiber design variable); and (ii) higher-order SSM terms
let steering flatten the backbone over a wider band once $\gamma_3$ is minimized.
We regard the honest quantification of the pure-steering lever as the primary
result and these as the natural next steps.

\section{The two-dimensional von K\'arm\'an plate}\label{sec:plate}

The beam showed the mechanism but bounded the lever. A two-dimensional laminated
plate has far more freedom --- a steered fiber \emph{field} and (later) a density
field --- and its anisotropy lets the tow angle retune the nonlinearity, not only
the frequency.

\subsection{Kinematics and energy}
For a symmetric laminate ($\bm B=\bm 0$) the plate carries transverse deflection
$w$ and in-plane displacements $u,v$. The von K\'arm\'an membrane strain and the
curvature are
\begin{equation}
\bm\varepsilon=\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}u_{,x}\\ v_{,y}\\ u_{,y}+v_{,x}\end{bmatrix}}_{\bm\varepsilon_L(u,v)}
+\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac12 w_{,x}^2\\ \tfrac12 w_{,y}^2\\ w_{,x}w_{,y}\end{bmatrix}}_{\bm\varepsilon_{NL}(w)},
\qquad
\bm\kappa=\big[-w_{,xx},\,-w_{,yy},\,-2w_{,xy}\big]^{\!\top},
\end{equation}
and the strain energy, with the fiber-angle-dependent membrane and bending
stiffnesses $\bm A(\theta),\bm D(\theta)$ from CLT, is
\begin{equation}
U=\tfrac12\!\int\!\bm\kappa^{\!\top}\bm D(\theta)\,\bm\kappa\,\dd\Omega
 +\underbrace{\tfrac12\!\int\!\bm\varepsilon_L^{\!\top}\bm A\,\bm\varepsilon_L}_{\text{in-plane stiffness}}
 +\underbrace{\int\!\bm\varepsilon_L^{\!\top}\bm A\,\bm\varepsilon_{NL}}_{\text{quadratic }u\!\leftrightarrow\! w^2}
 +\underbrace{\tfrac12\!\int\!\bm\varepsilon_{NL}^{\!\top}\bm A\,\bm\varepsilon_{NL}}_{\text{cubic }w^4}.
\label{eq:plate-energy}
\end{equation}
The last term is the membrane-stretching hardening; the middle term is the
\emph{quadratic} coupling that drives the in-plane fields from the transverse
motion.

\paragraph{Governing equations.} Stationarity of the Lagrangian --- kinetic energy
$T=\tfrac12\!\int\!\rho h\,\dot w^2\,\dd\Omega$ (plus in-plane inertia) minus $U$ ---
gives, in strong form, the laminated \emph{F\"oppl--von K\'arm\'an} plate equations.
With the membrane and bending stress resultants
$\bm N=\bm A(\theta)\,(\bm\varepsilon_L+\bm\varepsilon_{NL})$ and
$\bm M=\bm D(\theta)\,\bm\kappa$,
\begin{align}
N_{\alpha\beta,\beta}&=0,
\label{eq:fvk-in}\\[2pt]
M_{\alpha\beta,\alpha\beta}+\big(N_{\alpha\beta}\,w_{,\beta}\big)_{,\alpha}+p(\bm x,t)
&=\rho h\,\ddot w,
\label{eq:fvk-w}
\end{align}
the in-plane equilibrium~\eqref{eq:fvk-in} and the transverse
equation~\eqref{eq:fvk-w} coupled through the membrane force $\bm N$ acting on the
\emph{deflected} geometry --- the geometric (von K\'arm\'an) nonlinearity, cubic in
$w$ after the in-plane fields are eliminated. Steering makes
$\bm A(\theta),\bm D(\theta)$ --- and, off the principal axes, their bend--twist /
extension--shear ($16,26$) couplings --- vary with position. We do not solve
\eqref{eq:fvk-in}--\eqref{eq:fvk-w} in strong form but discretize the energy.

\subsection{Assumed-modes reduction and membrane relaxation}
Expand $w=\sum_i q^w_i\phi_i$ (simply-supported bending modes),
$u=\sum q^u_j\psi_j$ and $v=\sum q^v_k\psi_k$ (in-plane modes), collect the modal
coordinates into $\bm x=[\bm q^w;\bm q^u;\bm q^v]$, and apply Galerkin's method to
\eqref{eq:fvk-in}--\eqref{eq:fvk-w} (equivalently Lagrange's equations on the
quadrature-integrated energy). The field equations collapse to \emph{exactly} the
semidiscrete system~\eqref{eq:eom},
\begin{equation}
\bm M\ddot{\bm x}+\bm C\dot{\bm x}+\bm K(\theta)\,\bm x
+\bm B_2(\theta)[\bm x,\bm x]+\bm T_3(\theta)[\bm x,\bm x,\bm x]=\bm f(t),
\label{eq:plate-eom}
\end{equation}
whose operators are moments of the fiber-angle field over the domain:
$M_{ij}=\int\!\rho h\,\phi_i\phi_j$; the stiffness $\bm K(\theta)$ is block-diagonal
in bending $\int\!\bm\kappa_i^{\!\top}\bm D(\theta)\bm\kappa_j$ and in-plane membrane
$\int\!\bm\varepsilon_{L,i}^{\!\top}\bm A(\theta)\bm\varepsilon_{L,j}$; and the
nonlinear internal forces are the gradients of the nonlinear energy in
\eqref{eq:plate-energy},
\begin{equation}
\bm B_2(\theta)[\bm x,\bm x]=\partial_{\bm x}\!\!\int\!\bm\varepsilon_L^{\!\top}\bm A(\theta)\,\bm\varepsilon_{NL},
\qquad
\bm T_3(\theta)[\bm x,\bm x,\bm x]=\partial_{\bm x}\,\tfrac12\!\!\int\!\bm\varepsilon_{NL}^{\!\top}\bm A(\theta)\,\bm\varepsilon_{NL},
\label{eq:plate-multilinear}
\end{equation}
the quadratic $u\!\leftrightarrow\!w^2$ coupling and the cubic membrane-stretching
hardening respectively. Each multilinear form is extracted numerically at the Gauss
points (the symmetric $\bm B_2,\bm T_3$ identical to PyHB's model), so a spatially
varying $\theta(\bm x)$ enters every quadrature point exactly. Equation
\eqref{eq:plate-eom} is the discrete dynamical system that the SSM of
Sec.~\ref{sec:ssm} reduces --- $\bm C=2\zeta\omega_1\bm M$ supplies light modal
damping. The
master mode is the fundamental bending mode; the in-plane modes are two orders
of magnitude stiffer and are \emph{slaved} to it. Precisely, the order-2 SSM
manifold coefficients \eqref{eq:homological} carry the quadratic response of the
in-plane fields to $w^2$, and this slaved membrane relaxation feeds back through
the resonant coefficient \eqref{eq:gamma} to \emph{reduce} the cubic hardening
$\gamma_3$. Dropping the in-plane DOF (immovable edges) recovers a purely cubic
upper bound.

Figure~\ref{fig:relax} shows the effect. As in-plane modes are added the
hardening coefficient falls from the restrained value and converges (by three
modes per direction) to the relaxed value, a $33\%$ reduction; the reduced
backbone matches full-order EPMC to $<0.1\%$; and the slaved in-plane field is
the expected outward membrane flow beneath the transverse bulge. Because EPMC and
the time-domain checks share the assumed-modes basis, they validate the SSM
\emph{reduction} but not the \emph{discretization}; we therefore validate the
assumed-modes backbone independently against a finite-element model in
Appendix~\ref{app:fe}. The normalized detuning matches FE to $\sim\!2\%$ for a
strongly steered plate with an enriched expansion (the absolute frequency is a
slowly-converging Ritz upper bound), so the relative results reported here are
FE-accurate; a displacement-based von~K\'arm\'an FE would replace the assumed modes
throughout at higher cost, reduced by the same SSM.

\subsection{Why anisotropy retunes the hardening}\label{sec:why}
Unlike the beam, the plate laminate is \emph{symmetric} ($\bm B=\bm 0$): the
bending--extension coupling that levered the beam is gone. The lever here is the
anisotropy itself, and the mechanism is worth stating precisely because it is
what makes the two-dimensional problem qualitatively stronger.

The linear frequency is fixed by the bending functional
$\int\bm\kappa^{\!\top}\bm D(\theta)\bm\kappa$; the hardening $\gamma_3$ by the
(relaxed) membrane functional
$\int\bm\varepsilon_{NL}^{\!\top}\bm A(\theta)\bm\varepsilon_{NL}$ minus the
in-plane relaxation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gamma}. Both read the \emph{same} tow-angle
field --- and for a single-ply-like laminate $\bm D\!\propto\!\bm A\!\propto\!
\bar{\bm Q}(\theta)$ pointwise --- yet they sample it through \emph{different
operators}: bending through the curvature
$\bm\kappa=(w_{,xx},w_{,yy},2w_{,xy})$ (second derivatives, largest at the panel
center where the mode bulges), stretching through
$\bm\varepsilon_{NL}=\tfrac12(w_{,x}^2,w_{,y}^2,2w_{,x}w_{,y})$ (first-derivatives
squared, largest toward the edges where the slope peaks), and along different
tensor directions in the $(11,22,12)$ stiffness space.

Two consequences follow. First, an \emph{isotropic} material cannot separate the
two: a single modulus scales bending and membrane stiffness together --- the
two-dimensional form of the beam's $r=\sqrt{D/A}$ obstruction. Anisotropy
replaces that scalar with the direction-dependent tensor $\bar{\bm Q}(\theta)$,
so the fiber angle becomes a knob that trades bending against membrane stiffness
and redirects both. Second, a single \emph{global} angle moves the whole tensor
rigidly and cannot hold one functional while changing the other; a \emph{steered
field} has the spatial freedom to keep the bending integral (the frequency) on
its iso-surface while sliding along that surface to cut the membrane integral
(the hardening) --- fibers stiff where curvature sets the frequency (the center),
compliant to the stretch where slope-squared sets the hardening (the edges). The
membrane \emph{relaxation} of the previous subsection amplifies the lever: the
same anisotropic $\bm A(\theta)$ that stores the stretching energy also sets how
readily it relieves into in-plane flow, and steering opens compliant in-plane
paths that soften $\gamma_3$ further. This spatial mismatch --- between where the
bending energy lives and where the stretching lives --- is exactly the freedom the
one-dimensional beam lacked, and it is why the plate lever reaches tens of
percent where the beam's reached only a few.

\subsection{The tow-curvature manufacturability constraint}\label{sec:manuf}
Automated fiber placement (AFP) lays each ply as a set of continuous tow courses; the
head cannot steer a course tighter than a minimum in-plane radius $R_{\min}$ --- set by
tow width, gap/overlap tolerance, and out-of-plane wrinkling --- without defects. This
is the constraint that distinguishes a \emph{manufacturable} steered laminate from an
arbitrary orientation field, and it is enforced throughout. For an orientation field
$\theta(\bm x)$ the fiber direction is $\bm n=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$, and the curvature
of a tow \emph{path} --- how fast the fiber turns as one advances \emph{along} it --- is
the directional derivative of the angle along the fiber,
\begin{equation}
\kappa_{\mathrm{tow}}(\bm x)=(\bm n\!\cdot\!\nabla)\theta
=\cos\theta\,\theta_{,x}+\sin\theta\,\theta_{,y},
\label{eq:towcurv}
\end{equation}
so the local steering radius is $R=1/|\kappa_{\mathrm{tow}}|$ and the AFP limit
$R\ge R_{\min}$ is the \emph{pointwise} inequality
\begin{equation}
|\kappa_{\mathrm{tow}}(\bm x)|\le\zall\equiv 1/R_{\min}\qquad\forall\,\bm x .
\label{eq:curl}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:towcurv} is a curl-like first-order operator that is \emph{nonlinear}
in $\theta$ (through $\bm n=\bm n(\theta)$); it is the manufacturability constraint the
beam of Sec.~\ref{sec:results} and the plate below both carry.

\paragraph{Orientation field.} We represent $\theta$ by compactly-supported radial basis
functions (CS-RBF) seeded on a coarse set of support points,
$\theta(\bm x)=\bm\Phi(\bm x)\,\hat{\bm\theta}$, with the interpolation matrix
$\bm\Phi$ and its analytic gradients $\bm\Phi_x,\bm\Phi_y$ at least $C^2$, so that
$\kappa_{\mathrm{tow}}$ in~\eqref{eq:towcurv} is well defined; the compact support keeps
$\bm\Phi$ sparse and the field intrinsically smooth (hence printable). The design
variables are the coefficients $\hat{\bm\theta}$. The constraint~\eqref{eq:curl}, at each
evaluation point $e$, is the smooth function
\begin{equation}
\zeta_e(\hat{\bm\theta})=\cos(\bm\Phi_e\hat{\bm\theta})\,(\bm\Phi_{x,e}\hat{\bm\theta})
+\sin(\bm\Phi_e\hat{\bm\theta})\,(\bm\Phi_{y,e}\hat{\bm\theta}),\qquad \zeta_e^2\le\zall^2,
\label{eq:curlfield}
\end{equation}
whose Jacobian $\partial\zeta_e/\partial\hat{\bm\theta}=
[-\sin\theta_e(\bm\Phi_{x,e}\hat{\bm\theta})+\cos\theta_e(\bm\Phi_{y,e}\hat{\bm\theta})]\,\bm\Phi_e
+\cos\theta_e\,\bm\Phi_{x,e}+\sin\theta_e\,\bm\Phi_{y,e}$ is analytic. The same transpose
$\bm\Phi^{\!\top}$ that assembles this constraint gradient pulls back the objective
sensitivity, $\partial J/\partial\hat{\bm\theta}=\bm\Phi^{\!\top}\,\partial J/\partial\theta$
--- the orientation analogue of a filtered density field.

\paragraph{Constrained problem.} The manufacturable design problem is
\begin{equation}
\min_{\hat{\bm\theta}}\;J(\hat{\bm\theta})\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\zeta_e^2-\zall^2\le 0\;\;\forall e,\qquad
\underline{\omega}\le\omega_1(\hat{\bm\theta})\le\overline{\omega},
\label{eq:designprob}
\end{equation}
with $J$ the SSM backbone objective and the frequency band holding the linear frequency
so the gain is genuine nonlinear tailoring, not a softer plate (the two-field problem of
Sec.~\ref{sec:topopt} adds a volume equality on the density). We solve it by the method of
moving asymptotes (MMA), whose convex separable subproblems suit many \emph{local}
inequality constraints and a moderate number of variables, wrapping the pointwise
curl constraints in an augmented Lagrangian
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L=J+\sum_e\frac{1}{2\mu}\Big(\max(0,\;\lambda_e+\mu h_e)^2-\lambda_e^2\Big),
\qquad h_e=\zeta_e^2-\zall^2,
\label{eq:auglag}
\end{equation}
the standard augmented Lagrangian for inequality constraints, with multiplier update
$\lambda_e\!\leftarrow\!\max(0,\,\lambda_e+\mu h_e)$, a
geometrically-increasing penalty $\mu$ while infeasible, and retention of the best
feasible iterate. Its gradient combines the SSM adjoint (the objective) with the analytic
curl Jacobian (the constraint), so each step costs one reduced solve plus sparse
contractions --- independent of the number of design variables.

\subsection{Manufacturable design and the front}
The plate plugs into this framework unchanged: the CS-RBF orientation field
$\theta(\bm x)=\bm\Phi\hat{\bm\theta}$, the curl constraint~\eqref{eq:curl}, and MMA with
the augmented Lagrangian~\eqref{eq:auglag} and best-feasible retention. The backbone objective is the
frequency-normalized amplitude detuning $J=\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}/K_b$, where
$\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is the fundamental's \emph{relaxed} effective cubic --- the
real SSM backbone coefficient, formed in closed form by statically condensing the
(fast) in-plane degrees of freedom. Writing the modal membrane energy as
$\tfrac12 K_b q^2+\tfrac12\bm s^{\!\top}\bm K_{\mathrm{in}}\bm s
+q^2\bm g^{\!\top}\bm s+\tfrac14\,\mathrm{knl}\,q^4$ in the transverse amplitude
$q$ and in-plane amplitudes $\bm s$, minimizing over $\bm s$ gives
$\bm s^\star=-q^2\bm K_{\mathrm{in}}^{-1}\bm g$ and
\begin{equation}
\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}=\mathrm{knl}-2\,\bm g^{\!\top}\bm K_{\mathrm{in}}^{-1}\bm g
\;<\;\mathrm{knl},
\label{eq:gammaeff}
\end{equation}
the raw membrane cubic minus the relaxation. This closed form reproduces the
full multi-mode SSM backbone coefficient of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gamma} to four digits on
uniform layups (and within $\sim\!2\%$ under steering), so the design is driven by
the \emph{true} response-curve coefficient rather than a stiffness-ratio proxy.
Its sensitivity is the analytic adjoint
\begin{equation}
\frac{\dd\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}}{\dd\theta}
=\dd\,\mathrm{knl}-4(\dd\bm g)^{\!\top}\bm u^\star
+2\,\bm u^{\star\top}(\dd\bm K_{\mathrm{in}})\bm u^\star,
\qquad \bm u^\star=\bm K_{\mathrm{in}}^{-1}\bm g,
\label{eq:gammaeffadj}
\end{equation}
in which the single in-plane solve $\bm u^\star$ is reused across every design
degree of freedom; pulled back through the CS-RBF transpose
($\partial J/\partial\hat{\bm\theta}=\bm\Phi^{\!\top}\partial J/\partial\theta$)
it is verified against finite differences to $\sim\!10^{-8}$. This is the
response-curve sensitivity of \cite{pozzi2025,li2024} in the fiber-field setting.
Figure~\ref{fig:platefront} reports a first manufacturability front on the plate:
the achievable detuning reduction falls from the unconstrained optimum as the
tow-curvature limit tightens, and the optimized tow field is smooth and printable.
Here the two-dimensional field is a far stronger lever than the one-dimensional
beam (tens of percent vs.\ a few).

\paragraph{Confirmation on the real plate, three ways.} We confirm the optimized
design on the physically complete model, against two independent ground truths.
Transferring the curl-feasible optimized tow layout onto the multi-mode von
K\'arm\'an plate \emph{with in-plane relaxation}, and holding the linear frequency
through the $\bm K_b$ ceiling (within $+5\%$; $f_1=270$ vs.\ $256\,\si{Hz}$), the
SSM backbone matches full-order EPMC continuation to $\sim\!1\%$ for \emph{both}
designs, and the relative amplitude-detuning reduction is $50\%$ (SSM and EPMC
agree). Beyond the
backbone, the SSM predicts the full \emph{damped forced response}: seeking a
periodic response $p_1=a\,e^{i\Om t}$ on the manifold reduces the forced dynamics
to $[i\Om-s(\rho)]\,a=f_1/2$ with modal forcing $f_1=\bm u_1^{*}[\bm f;\bm 0]$,
giving the leading-order frequency-response curve
$[(\Om-\Om(\rho))^2+\alpha(\rho)^2]\,\rho^2=|f_1|^2$ whose peak rides the backbone
$\Om=\Om(\rho)$. Direct time-domain frequency sweeps of the full nonlinear plate
(RK45, in-plane statically condensed, $2\%$ Rayleigh damping) --- run up and down
to expose the hardening jump and hysteresis --- trace steady-state peaks that land
on the SSM forced-response curve (Fig.~\ref{fig:plateconfirm}), and steering
visibly flattens the resonance at held frequency. The result is therefore not an
artifact of the reduction: it holds against harmonic-balance \emph{and}
brute-force time integration.

Because the design is driven by $\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}$
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:gammaeff}), which \emph{equals} the full SSM's backbone coefficient
to four digits, the optimizer's own metric and this verified reduction coincide:
there is no surrogate gap. An earlier stiffness-ratio objective
$J=k_{nl}/K_b$, by contrast, is blind to the $\theta$-dependent membrane
relaxation and to the modal adjoint weighting --- it moved only $\sim\!6\%$ on the
designs whose real detuning dropped by half --- which is precisely why the design
must be driven by the SSM's own coefficient and its adjoint
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:gammaeffadj}), not a proxy. The remaining lever, and the immediate
next step, is the density/topology field $\bm z$ --- the second wavetopo design
variable --- reduced by the same SSM.

\subsection{Forced response, the fold, and the jump-free amplitude}\label{sec:frc}
The backbone is the free (self-sustained) response; the jump lives on the \emph{forced}
response, which the same reduction delivers at leading order. Adding harmonic modal
forcing $\bm f\cos\Omega t$ to the equation of motion~\eqref{eq:eom} and projecting onto
the SSM, the steady amplitude $\rho$ of the directly-driven mode satisfies the algebraic
forced-response relation
\begin{equation}
\big[(\Omega-\omega(\rho))^2+\alpha(\rho)^2\big]\,\rho^2=|f_1|^2,\qquad
f_1=\bm u_1^{*}\,[\bm f;\,\bm0],
\label{eq:frc}
\end{equation}
in which $\omega(\rho)$ and $\alpha(\rho)$ are the \emph{same} backbone frequency and
amplitude-dependent damping as the free response~\eqref{eq:backbone}
---$\omega(\rho)=\Imag\!\big(\lambda_1+\sum_k\gamma_{2k+1}(\rho/2)^{2k}\big)$,
$\alpha(\rho)=\Real(\cdots)$--- and the drive enters only through its projection $f_1$
onto the master left eigenvector. The physical forced-response curve (FRC) follows by
sampling $\bm x=[\bm I\;\bm0]\bm W(\bm p)$; Eq.~\eqref{eq:frc} is a resonance peak that
\emph{leans} in the direction the backbone bends and folds once it leans past vertical.

\paragraph{The fold and the jump-free amplitude.} Solving~\eqref{eq:frc} for the drive
frequency,
\begin{equation}
\Omega_\pm(\rho)=\omega(\rho)\pm\sqrt{|f_1|^2/\rho^2-\alpha(\rho)^2},
\label{eq:frcbranch}
\end{equation}
the response is multivalued in $\Omega$ --- and a swept excitation \emph{jumps} between
branches with hysteresis --- exactly where $\dd\Omega/\dd\rho=0$ acquires a pair of roots
(a saddle-node fold). The smallest drive at which a fold first appears sets the
\emph{jump-free amplitude} $a_{\mathrm c}$: below it the resonance is single-valued and
hysteresis-free. For a hardening backbone $\omega(\rho)\simeq\omega_1+\kappa\,a^2$ with
$\kappa=\dd\Omega/\dd A^2$ and light damping $\alpha\simeq\zeta\omega_1$, the fold first
forms where the backbone's frequency shift overtakes the damping bandwidth,
$\kappa\,a_{\mathrm c}^2\sim\zeta\omega_1$, i.e.
\begin{equation}
a_{\mathrm c}\;\sim\;\sqrt{\zeta\,\omega_1/\kappa}\;\propto\;\kappa^{-1/2}.
\label{eq:ac}
\end{equation}
Halving the hardening coefficient $\kappa$ at held $\omega_1$ therefore widens the
jump-free range by $\sqrt2$ --- precisely the lever the plate design pulls. We do not
depend on the estimate~\eqref{eq:ac}: in Sec.~\ref{sec:jump} $a_{\mathrm c}$ is computed
exactly as the drive at which~\eqref{eq:frc} first turns multivalued. The point is that
the forced response, its fold, and the jump-free amplitude are all algebraic in the
\emph{same} coefficients $\gamma_{2k+1}$ the design already optimizes --- so shaping the
backbone shapes the jump.

\subsection{The engineering payoff: a wider jump-free operating range}
\label{sec:jump}
A halved detuning coefficient is a means, not the end; its consequence is what a
resonant device actually cares about. Above a critical drive the hardening
forced-response curve \emph{folds}, and the response jumps --- a sudden amplitude
change with hysteresis that corrupts a resonant sensor or harvester and can be
unsafe. The jump is the saddle-node fold of Sec.~\ref{sec:frc}, and by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ac} its onset amplitude scales as $a_{\mathrm{c}}\!\sim\!\sqrt{\zeta\omega_1/\kappa}
\propto\kappa^{-1/2}$, so halving the hardening $\kappa$ raises $a_{\mathrm{c}}$ by
$\sqrt2$ at held frequency. We compute $a_{\mathrm{c}}$ directly, as the
drive at which the forced-response curve~\eqref{eq:frc} first becomes multivalued: on our panel
($\zeta=2\%$) the steered tow lifts the jump-free amplitude from $a/h=0.208$ to
$0.296$ --- a $1.42\times$ wider amplitude range, equivalently $\sim\!1.7\times$ more
drive before the resonance becomes multivalued (Fig.~\ref{fig:jump}). At any drive
between the two thresholds
the unidirectional panel's resonance folds and jumps while the steered panel stays
single-valued: the steering has \emph{removed the jump}. The two-field topology
design pushes this further, to $\sim\!2.4\times$ the jump-free amplitude. This is the
tangible result --- a composite resonator that stays linear, jump- and
hysteresis-free, to substantially larger amplitude, purely by how the tows are laid.

We confirm the jump itself by brute force. Sweeping a harmonic drive up and then
down through the resonance --- direct RK45 time integration of the full nonlinear

$0.054\,h$ across the fold. The steered panel's up- and down-sweeps coincide to
$0.009\,h$: no jump (Fig.~\ref{fig:jumpnum}). The time-domain sweeps also track the
SSM forced-response curve point for point, closing the loop between the reduced
model that designs the panel and the full simulation that a test would measure.

\subsection{Engineering utility: which devices this buys}\label{sec:utility}
A backbone coefficient is an abstraction; the reason to halve it is that three
concrete device requirements follow directly from it, each of which a plain
composite panel fails to meet at working amplitude.

\emph{A resonant sensor that stays calibrated.} Resonant mass, gas, and strain
sensors read a frequency shift as their signal, so any dependence of the operating
frequency on drive amplitude injects a systematic error --- amplitude noise
masquerades as signal and the calibration drifts as the excitation level changes.
The detuning we minimize \emph{is} that amplitude--frequency coupling; holding the
linear frequency while cutting the hardening (Eq.~\eqref{eq:gammaeff}) decouples the
read-out from the drive level, extending the linear operating range without a new
material, a larger proof mass, or a re-calibration.

\emph{A vibration energy harvester with usable bandwidth.} A harvester wants the
largest response over the widest band of excitation frequency. A hardening
resonance leans over and folds, so its effective bandwidth is capped by the
saddle-node: past the fold the amplitude collapses to the lower branch and the
harvested power drops abruptly. Widening the jump-free amplitude by $1.42\times$
(and by $\sim\!2.4\times$ with the added topology field, Sec.~\ref{sec:topopt}) is
directly a wider high-amplitude operating band, obtained from the layup rather than
from a tuned proof mass or a softer, heavier structure.

\emph{A hysteresis-free actuator, filter, or MEMS resonator.} Where the forced
response is multivalued the device is hysteretic: a sweep up and a sweep down give
different steady amplitudes at the same frequency (Fig.~\ref{fig:jumpnum}), which is
fatal for an element run in closed loop or used as a frequency reference. Removing
the jump makes the amplitude single-valued and the response repeatable --- the
baseline requirement for a resonant actuator or mechanical filter, here met by tow
placement alone.

The thread common to all three is what makes fiber steering distinctive as a design
lever. Every classical composite knob --- ply count, stacking sequence, added mass,
stiffeners --- moves the linear frequency and the part mass together with any change
to the nonlinearity. Steering is the one knob that reshapes the \emph{nonlinear}
response at \emph{fixed} linear frequency and fixed mass, and it does so with
automated-fiber-placement hardware and a prepreg already in the shop: the design
cost is a curvature-feasible tow path, not a change to the bill of materials.
Moreover, the same adjoint that \emph{minimizes} the detuning can \emph{prescribe}
it (Sec.~\ref{sec:points}, Fig.~\ref{fig:points}), so the utility is not merely
``flatter'' but a resonator whose amplitude--frequency law is specified at design
time and printed into the fiber path. What steering cannot do is exceed the physical
lever: on a clamped beam the gain is a few percent (Sec.~\ref{sec:results}), and even
on the plate it is a factor, not an order of magnitude --- the honest bound recorded
throughout Secs.~\ref{sec:results} and \ref{sec:plate}. Within that bound, the
deliverable is a manufacturable composite resonator whose nonlinear operating
envelope is a design output rather than an accident of the layup.

\subsection{Dial-a-backbone: designing the toolpath to a target}
Minimizing the detuning is one use of the adjoint; \emph{prescribing} it is
another, and it is the manufacturable analogue of the forced-response-curve design
of \cite{liang2025,pozzi2025}. Replacing the objective with a least-squares match
to a target relative detuning $\tau=J/J_{[0]}$ (the design's frequency-normalized
hardening as a fraction of the unidirectional baseline's), the \emph{same} tow-field
adjoint steers a single panel to a \emph{specified} nonlinearity. Figure~\ref{fig:tune}
drives one panel to $\tau=0.80,\,0.65,\,0.50$; the achieved values land on the
targets to $\sim\!0.01$, at held linear frequency and curl-feasibly, and the toolpath
visibly steers harder for a deeper target (minimum tow radius $73\!\to\!30$ mm --- the
manufacturing cost of a stronger nonlinear correction). The panel becomes a
\emph{tunable} nonlinear element: a set of such panels, each dialed to spec, is a
graded array of nonlinear resonators or matched absorbers.

\section{Two fields: topology \emph{and} fiber steering}\label{sec:topopt}

The orientation field decides which way the tows run; the second wavetopo design
field --- the structural \emph{density} $\bm z$ --- decides where material goes at
all. Adding it turns the problem into genuine topology optimization coupled to
fiber steering, and it plugs into the same SSM backbone objective.

\subsection{Three-field density and the density-weighted backbone}
We use the standard density parametrization: a raw variable $z_e\in[0,1]$ per
element, a linear \emph{filter} $\bm y_f=\bm P\bm z$ (radius $R_\rho$, imposing a
minimum feature size), a smooth \emph{Heaviside projection}
$y=\tfrac{\tanh(\beta\eta)+\tanh(\beta(y_f-\eta))}{\tanh(\beta\eta)+\tanh(\beta(1-\eta))}$
that crisps the topology toward $0/1$ as $\beta$ is continued upward, and a SIMP
interpolation $E(y)=\varepsilon+(1-\varepsilon)y^{p}$ ($p=3$) of the element
stiffness. Every element quadratic form of Sec.~\ref{sec:plate} is scaled by
$E_e$: the bending $K_b=\sum_e E_e\,k^b_e$, the raw cubic
$\mathrm{knl}=\sum_e E_e\,k^{nl}_e$, the in-plane stiffness
$\bm K_{\mathrm{in}}=\sum_e E_e\,\bm K^{\mathrm{in}}_e$ and the coupling
$\bm g=\sum_e E_e\,\bm g_e$, so the relaxed backbone coefficient
$\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}=\mathrm{knl}-2\bm g^{\!\top}\bm K_{\mathrm{in}}^{-1}\bm g$
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:gammaeff}) becomes a function of \emph{both} fields. The modal mass
$M=\rho h\sum_e y_e A_e\phi_e^2$ scales linearly with the physical density, so the
linear frequency $\omega_1=\sqrt{K_b/M}$ is available for the constraint below.

\subsection{Coupled sensitivities}
The design vector is $(\bm z,\hat{\bm\theta})$ and the objective is again
$J=\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}/K_b$. The orientation sensitivity is Eq.~\eqref{eq:gammaeffadj}
with each element term now carrying its weight $E_e$. The density sensitivity
threads the three-field chain: with $\bm u^\star=\bm K_{\mathrm{in}}^{-1}\bm g$,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}}{\partial E_e}
=k^{nl}_e-4\,\bm g_e^{\!\top}\bm u^\star+2\,\bm u^{\star\top}\bm K^{\mathrm{in}}_e\bm u^\star,
\qquad
\frac{\dd J}{\dd\bm z}=\bm P^{\!\top}\!\Big[\frac{\partial J}{\partial E}\,
\frac{\dd E}{\dd y}\,\frac{\dd y}{\dd y_f}\Big],
\label{eq:dzchain}
\end{equation}
where $\partial J/\partial E=\partial_E\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}/K_b-\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}K_b^{-2}k^b_e$,
$\dd E/\dd y=(1-\varepsilon)p\,y^{p-1}$ and $\dd y/\dd y_f$ is the Heaviside
derivative --- and the single adjoint solve $\bm u^\star$ is reused across every
$z_e$ and every $\hat\theta_j$. Both sensitivities are verified against central
finite differences to $\lesssim\!10^{-7}$; the orientation sensitivity also passes
the \emph{isotropy-annihilation} test --- setting the lamina stiffness isotropic
makes the tow angle inert and drives $\partial J/\partial\hat{\bm\theta}$ to
$\sim\!10^{-9}$, confirming the whole angular chain.

\subsection{Constrained optimization}
We drive $(\bm z,\hat{\bm\theta})$ with MMA under an augmented-Lagrangian for three
constraints: the per-element tow-curvature limit $\zeta_e^2\le\zall^2$ (orientation
only), a material-volume budget $\overline{y}=v_{\mathrm{all}}$ as an
\emph{equality} (a fixed budget --- without it the scale-near-invariant $J$ would
drive material trivially to zero), and a linear-frequency \emph{band}
$f_{\mathrm{lo}}\,\omega_1^{\mathrm{ref}}\le\omega_1\le f_{\mathrm{hi}}\,\omega_1^{\mathrm{ref}}$.
The band's \emph{ceiling} is essential: with a floor alone the optimizer discovers
the spurious low-density localized mode where the SIMP $\varepsilon$-stiffness with
vanishing mass sends $\omega_1\!\to\!\infty$ and $\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}\!\to\!0$
trivially, giving a disconnected sliver design; the ceiling forbids it. Because the
$(1,1)$ mode is symmetric about both centre-lines we project $\bm z$ onto the
quarter-symmetric subspace, which selects the physical optimum over lopsided local
minima.

\subsection{Result}
Figure~\ref{fig:topopt} shows the converged two-field design at a held frequency
and a fixed $50\%$ material budget. The topology is connected and symmetric, and it
voids material exactly at the top- and bottom-edge midpoints --- the antinodes of the
membrane stretch $(\nabla w)^2$, where the hardening integrand
$\bm\varepsilon_{NL}^{\!\top}\bm A\bm\varepsilon_{NL}$ concentrates --- while keeping
the high-curvature centre that carries the frequency; the tows then steer
\emph{around} the voids, curl-feasibly. The first-mode backbone drift falls from
$17.1\%$ to $3.0\%$ ($-83\%$), extending the orientation-only lever.

\paragraph{Confirmation on the adapted mode.} A single fixed master mode cannot
reshape around the new voids, so one might expect it to over-credit topology. It
does not: transferring the optimized topology onto the \emph{real multi-mode}
plate --- SIMP-weighting its stiffness and mass at every quadrature point so the
eigenmode itself adapts to the perforations --- the SSM detuning reduction is
$82\%$, against the reduced model's $83\%$. The relative-detuning lever therefore
\emph{survives} mode adaptation; it is not a fixed-mode artifact. One caveat we
state plainly: the single-mode frequency is a loose proxy for the real multi-mode
frequency of a strongly-steered design (the tow field alone reads far stiffer on
the multi-mode plate, and the voids bring the frequency back to $0.94\times$
baseline), so the topology design's held-frequency rests on a cancellation rather
than a tight constraint --- constraining the real multi-mode frequency directly is
the refinement. The contribution here is that the framework carries \emph{both}
manufacturable fields --- orientation and topology --- on the same verified SSM
backbone objective and its adjoint, and that the topology lever holds up against
the adapted-mode ground truth.

\section{Designing the backbone to spec on the adapted mode}\label{sec:points}

The one caveat left standing (Sec.~\ref{sec:topopt}) was that a \emph{fixed} master
mode mistracks the linear frequency of a strongly-steered design. We close it with
a \emph{mode-adaptive} reduction and use it to design the backbone to a target.

\subsection{The adapted-mode reduction and its analytic gradient}
Rather than freeze the master mode, take it to be the fundamental of the multi-mode
Ritz eigenproblem $\bm K_b(\theta)\bm v=\omega_1^2\bm M\bm v$ over a small transverse
basis $\{\phi_k\}$; the mode $w=\sum_k v_k\phi_k$ then reshapes with the design and
$\omega_1$ is the \emph{real} multi-mode frequency. The relaxed cubic
$\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:gammaeff}) is evaluated on this adapted mode,
and the objective is $J=\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}/(\omega_1^2\,m_c^2)$ with $m_c$ the
mode's midspan value (which fixes the physical amplitude normalization). The
gradient is fully analytic and FD-verified to $\sim\!10^{-7}$: the eigenvalue
(frequency) sensitivity is Fox--Kapoor and needs no eigenvector derivative, while the
two eigenvector-sensitivity terms --- of $\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}$ and of $m_c$ ---
each collapse to a \emph{single adjoint curvature field}
$\kappa_\beta=\sum_{j\ne0}\beta_j\,\kappa_{v_j}$, so the whole sensitivity costs the
one eigensolve plus cheap contractions.

\subsection{Threading the backbone through design points}
With both knobs in hand --- the real $\omega_1$ and the hardening curvature, each with
a verified gradient --- we can \emph{place} the backbone: minimizing the squared
mismatch to prescribed $(\,f_k,\,a_k)$ points in the frequency--amplitude plane steers
the tow field so the real backbone threads them. A caution the plate enforces: its
cubic backbone is a two-parameter family (a linear frequency and one hardening
curvature), so admissible design points must lie on \emph{one} achievable, positively
sloped hardening curve --- a vertical (iso-frequency-across-amplitude) target is not a
backbone a hardening plate can produce. Figure~\ref{fig:points} designs an
amplitude-stable resonator mandated near $335$ Hz: the toolpath threads three points
on a gentle-hardening target to an rms error of $3.3$ Hz ($\sim\!1\%$), curl-feasibly,
far flatter and higher than the unidirectional baseline. Crucially, the adapted-mode
frequency ($332$ Hz) agrees with the full multi-mode plate ($337$ Hz) to $1.5\%$ ---
where the old fixed-mode proxy erred by $70\%$ --- so the shipped device actually sits
at its mandated frequency. This is the manufacturable, frequency-honest version of
the response-curve design of \cite{pozzi2025,liang2025}.

\section{Designing an instability boundary: supersonic panel flutter}\label{sec:flutter}

The backbone is one target the framework exposes; the \emph{same} machinery designs a
\emph{stability boundary}. A thin panel in supersonic flow loses stability by flutter --- a
Hopf bifurcation of the flat state into self-excited oscillation --- and keeping that boundary
above the flight envelope is a hard constraint on every high-speed skin panel. Fiber steering
is the natural manufacturable lever (this extends the variable-stiffness tailoring of
\cite{gurdal,ijsselmuiden} from linear objectives to a nonlinear instability), and the
geometric nonlinearity sets what happens \emph{past} the boundary.

\subsection{Aeroelastic model and calibration}
First-order piston theory gives the aerodynamic pressure on a panel in a stream of Mach
number $M_\infty$ and dynamic pressure $q$; projected on the transverse modes it adds a
\emph{nonsymmetric} aerodynamic stiffness (the streamwise slope term) to the bending block,
\begin{equation}
G_{ij}=\!\int_\Omega\!\phi_i\,\partial_x\phi_j\,\dd\Omega,\qquad
\bm M\ddot{\bm q}+\bm C\dot{\bm q}+\big(\bm K_b(\theta)+\lambda_q\bm G\big)\bm q=\bm 0,
\quad \lambda_q=\tfrac{2q}{\sqrt{M_\infty^2-1}},
\label{eq:flutter-eom}
\end{equation}
plus light damping $\bm C$. Equation~\eqref{eq:flutter-eom} is a nonsymmetric generalized
eigenproblem; as $q$ rises two modes coalesce in frequency and a complex-conjugate pair of the
first-order pencil crosses the imaginary axis at the critical $q_{\mathrm{cr}}$ ---
\emph{coalescence flutter}. The implementation reproduces the classical result: a
one-dimensional simply-supported panel gives the nondimensional critical dynamic pressure
$\lambda_{\mathrm{cr}}=343.35$ against the textbook $343.36$ \cite{shaw}.

\subsection{Conforming discretization: why the assumed modes fail here}\label{sec:flutter-fe}
Unlike the backbone, the flutter boundary is \emph{not} adequately resolved by the global sine
modes. A steered --- or merely rotated --- laminate develops bend--twist couplings $D_{16},D_{26}$
that \emph{twist} the mode shapes; flutter is a coalescence of two modes and is exquisitely sensitive
to their shape, and the sine basis, which cannot represent the twist, gives the wrong coalescence. We
established this against an independent \emph{conforming} Bogner--Fox--Schmit (BFS) bicubic-Hermite
Kirchhoff element (nodal DOF $w,w_{,x},w_{,y},w_{,xy}$; frequencies exact against the closed form as in
App.~\ref{app:fe}): the two discretizations \emph{agree} while $D_{16}\!\approx\!0$
($0^\circ$--$5^\circ$) and \emph{diverge monotonically} as the coupling grows --- by a factor of
\emph{ten} at $30^\circ$, where the sine model over-predicts $q_{\mathrm{cr}}$ tenfold. The flutter
results below therefore use the conforming BFS element, and the sine-mode flutter is discarded. (The
backbone results of Secs.~\ref{sec:plate}--\ref{sec:points} are unaffected: those are frequencies and
detuning \emph{ratios}, FE-accurate to $\sim\!2\%$, App.~\ref{app:fe}.)

\subsection{Steering the boundary out}
On the conforming model the flutter boundary is a strong, \emph{non-monotone} function of the ply
angle: bend--twist coupling is maximally destabilizing near $30^\circ$--$40^\circ$, where a uniform
CFRP panel's $q_{\mathrm{cr}}$ collapses into a ``flutter hole'' an order of magnitude below the
flow-aligned $[0]$ value (Fig.~\ref{fig:flutter}a). A required operating frequency that places a
uniform layup in that hole is exactly where steering helps: the linear frequency and the flutter
boundary read the tow field through different operators, so a steered \emph{field} can hold the
frequency while placing the twist where it stabilizes rather than destabilizes. We maximize
$q_{\mathrm{cr}}$ at held $f_1$ and mass, curl-feasibly, driving the search by the boundary's analytic
adjoint --- the implicit eigenvalue derivative
\begin{equation}
\frac{\dd q_{\mathrm{cr}}}{\dd\theta}=-\frac{\mathrm{Re}(\dd s/\dd\theta)}{\mathrm{Re}(\dd s/\dd q)},
\qquad
\frac{\dd s}{\dd p}=\frac{\bm u^{*}(\partial_p\bm{\mathcal A})\,\bm v}{\bm u^{*}\bm{\mathcal B}\,\bm v},
\label{eq:flutter-adj}
\end{equation}
with $\bm u,\bm v$ the left/right eigenvectors of the critical mode of the (reduced) nonsymmetric
aeroelastic pencil --- the same left-eigenvector projection as the SSM adjoint (Eq.~\eqref{eq:gamma}),
the reduction being built on a general pencil that never assumed self-adjointness. Formed on the
conforming FE (reduced on its own modes, the modal-basis sensitivity absorbed by refreshing the basis
each design step), it is FD-verified to $2.1\times10^{-7}$. On a $200\times150\times2\,\si{mm}$ CFRP
panel at $M_\infty=2$ and a held $f_1=300\,\si{Hz}$ --- where the best uniform layup flutters at only
$\sim\!0.37\,\si{MPa}$ --- a curl-feasible steered field \textbf{recovers the flutter boundary to
$3.4\,\si{MPa}$, a $9\times$ margin} at held frequency and mass
(Figs.~\ref{fig:fluttermech}b, \ref{fig:flutter}b). Steering pushes the bending--torsion coalescence
from $q_{\mathrm{cr}}=0.37$ to $3.4\,\si{MPa}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:fluttermech}a), sliding the growth-rate
zero-crossing --- the Hopf onset --- the same $9\times$ to the right (Fig.~\ref{fig:fluttermech}b). The
lever is the manufacturable placement of the same bend--twist coupling that, uncontrolled, craters the
uniform panel.

\subsection{The nonlinear aftermath}
Past the boundary the geometric nonlinearity sets the consequence. Restoring the von~K\'arm\'an
relaxed cubic and reducing the flutter mode onto its Stuart--Landau normal form
$\dot p=\lambda(q)\,p+\gamma\,p|p|^2$, the cubic coefficient --- formed from the same left/right
flutter eigenvectors and the von~K\'arm\'an cubic tensor by the $\bm u_1^{*}$ projection --- has
$\mathrm{Re}\,\gamma<0$: the Hopf is \emph{supercritical}, so crossing it yields a benign, bounded
limit-cycle oscillation rather than a catastrophic subcritical jump (Fig.~\ref{fig:fluttermech}c). This criticality --- the sign of
$\mathrm{Re}\,\gamma$ --- is the cheap, model-robust output of the reduction. The finite limit-cycle
amplitude on the conforming model, and its reduction by steering, are a direct follow-on (our earlier
assumed-modes limit-cycle amplitudes are superseded along with the boundary they rested on).

\subsection{Relation to the framework}
Flutter uses no new reduction machinery --- the nonsymmetric aerodynamic operator drops into the same
first-order pencil, and the boundary sensitivity~\eqref{eq:flutter-adj} is the same left-eigenvector
projection as the backbone adjoint --- but it did force a \emph{conforming} discretization, and that
rigor is the point: an independent FE cross-check overturned a plausible-looking sine-mode result and
replaced a spurious $2\times$ with a correct, larger $9\times$ recovery. Linear flutter tailoring is
itself classical aeroelasticity; the contribution here is its manufacturable, adjoint-driven form on a
discretization honest enough to trust.

\section{Full-order optimization by EPMC, and its relation to the SSM adjoint}
\label{sec:epmc}

Everything above optimizes a \emph{reduced} backbone. The natural full-order
alternative is to optimize the backbone that harmonic balance computes directly,
with no reduction --- the extended periodic motion concept (EPMC)
\cite{krack2015}. We give its design sensitivity here, both because it is the
independent ground truth against which we validate the SSM (Sec.~\ref{sec:points}
and below) and because it is the engine one would use where the SSM truncation is
inadequate.

\subsection{EPMC and its harmonic-balance residual}
EPMC augments the autonomous equation of motion with a self-exciting,
mass-proportional term that offsets dissipation so that a genuine periodic orbit
--- the nonlinear mode --- persists:
\begin{equation}
\bm M\ddot{\bm q}+\big(\bm C-2\delta\Omega\,\bm M\big)\dot{\bm q}
+\bm K\bm q+\bm f_{\mathrm{nl}}(\bm q)=\bm 0 ,
\label{eq:epmc-eom}
\end{equation}
in which the artificial factor $\delta$ equals, at convergence, the mode's
amplitude-dependent damping ratio. Expanding the periodic motion in $H$ harmonics,
$\bm q(t)=a\sum_{k}\big(\bm\psi^{c}_{k}\cos k\Omega t+\bm\psi^{s}_{k}\sin k\Omega t\big)$
with $a$ the modal amplitude and $\bm\psi$ the unit-normalised shape, harmonic
balance gives the augmented residual in the unknowns $\bm z=(\bm\psi,\Omega,\delta)$,
\begin{equation}
\bm R(\bm z;a,\theta)=
\begin{bmatrix}
\bm S(\Omega,\delta;\theta)\,\bm\psi+a^{-1}\bm F_{\mathrm{nl}}(a\bm\psi;\theta)\\[2pt]
\bm\psi_1^{\!\top}\bm M\bm\psi_1-1\\[2pt]
(\psi^{s}_{1})_{\mathrm{ref}}
\end{bmatrix}=\bm 0,
\label{eq:epmc-res}
\end{equation}
where $\bm S$ is the block dynamic stiffness (per harmonic $k$: $\bm K-k^2\Omega^2\bm M$
on the diagonal, $k\Omega(\bm C-2\delta\Omega\bm M)$ coupling the cosine and sine
blocks), $\bm F_{\mathrm{nl}}$ is the alternating-frequency--time Fourier force, and
the last two rows fix the amplitude normalisation and the phase. Pseudo-arclength
continuation in $a$ traces the backbone $\Omega(a)$ and the modal damping $\delta(a)$
of the \emph{full} model.

\subsection{Design sensitivity of the EPMC backbone}
At a converged point $\bm R(\bm z;a,\theta)=0$; differentiating at fixed amplitude
and invoking the implicit function theorem,
\begin{equation}
\bm J_z\,\frac{\partial\bm z}{\partial\theta}=-\frac{\partial\bm R}{\partial\theta},
\qquad \bm J_z=\frac{\partial\bm R}{\partial\bm z},
\label{eq:epmc-ift}
\end{equation}
where $\bm J_z$ is exactly the EPMC Jacobian already assembled for the Newton
corrector. For a full tow-angle field one takes the backbone-frequency component
by an adjoint,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\dd\Omega}{\dd\theta}=-\bm w^{\!\top}\frac{\partial\bm R}{\partial\theta},
\qquad \bm J_z^{\!\top}\bm w=\bm e_\Omega ,
\label{eq:epmc-adj}
\end{equation}
one adjoint solve per amplitude. The design enters only through the last block,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial\bm R}{\partial\theta}=
\Big[\,(\partial_\theta\bm S)\bm\psi+a^{-1}\partial_\theta\bm F_{\mathrm{nl}}\,;\;
\bm\psi_1^{\!\top}(\partial_\theta\bm M)\bm\psi_1\,;\;0\,\Big],
\label{eq:epmc-dtheta}
\end{equation}
with $\partial_\theta\bm S$ built from $\partial_\theta\bm K$ (and, if the topology
field is active, $\partial_\theta\bm M$), and $\partial_\theta\bm F_{\mathrm{nl}}$ the
alternating-frequency--time transform of the fiber-angle derivative of the membrane
force. Crucially, these are the \emph{same} per-station $\dd\bar{\bm Q}/\dd\theta$
sensitivities that drive the CLT and SSM gradients; pulled back through the CS-RBF
transpose, Eqs.~\eqref{eq:epmc-adj}--\eqref{eq:epmc-dtheta} give a full-order
optimizer for the nonlinear backbone (and, with the forced residual in place of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:epmc-res}, for forced-response objectives).

\subsection{Relation to the SSM adjoint, and when to use which}
The SSM yields $\Omega(a)$ and $\dd\Omega/\dd\theta$ from the reduced coefficients
$\gamma_{2k+1}$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:gamma}) in closed form --- no periodic solve, no
continuation, and at a cost independent of both amplitude and harmonic count. Because
the SSM backbone \emph{coincides} with the EPMC backbone within the SSM's convergence
radius (Fig.~\ref{fig:epmcssm}): the two curves overlie one another for both the
baseline and the optimized plate up to $a/h\!\approx\!0.35$, and the full-order EPMC
confirms that the steered toolpath \emph{halves} the amplitude detuning at every
amplitude ($10.5\%\!\to\!5.3\%$ at $a/h\!=\!0.4$). Their design sensitivities agree
there, and the SSM is orders of magnitude cheaper --- a $0.28$ s reduced backbone
versus a $\sim\!325$ s full-order continuation, a factor of $\sim\!10^{3}$ --- one
reduced linear-algebra pass versus a full-order Newton solve at every continuation
step plus a per-amplitude adjoint. The cubic SSM does drift above EPMC past
$a/h\!\approx\!0.4$ (it slightly over-hardens), which is exactly where a higher-order
SSM or EPMC itself takes over. EPMC
remains the method of record beyond that radius --- large amplitude, strong
higher-harmonic content, internal resonances --- where an SSM would demand high
polynomial order. The two are therefore complementary: the SSM adjoint for cheap
gradient-based design in the polynomial regime, EPMC for independent validation and
for the strongly nonlinear or internally-resonant regime; and because
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:epmc-adj}--\eqref{eq:epmc-dtheta} reuse the identical
$\dd\bar{\bm Q}/\dd\theta$ chain, moving between them costs no new sensitivity code.

\subsection{Head-to-head on the Pozzi benchmark}
To compare the two engines as \emph{optimizers}, we solve the same design problem
with each on the published von~K\'arm\'an-beam backbone-shaping benchmark of
\cite{pozzi2025} (their Sec.~5.2 / Fig.~3): a clamped--clamped beam whose backbone
is shaped from hardening to a prescribed \emph{softening} by an initial curvature
$y=A_1\sin(\pi x/L)$. The straight beam hardens ($+2.2\%$ relative detuning at
$\mathrm{RMS}=0.2h_0$); the curvature injects a quadratic nonlinearity whose slaved
response drives the SSM cubic coefficient negative, softening the backbone (it also
stiffens the beam linearly, so the curvature-invariant measure is the detuning
relative to each design's \emph{own} $\omega_1$). We design $A_1$ to hit a $-1.5\%$
softening target, driving the optimizer once with the SSM backbone and once with the
full-order EPMC backbone. The two agree closely --- $A_1=8.36$ mm (SSM) versus
$8.26$ mm (EPMC), a $1.2\%$ difference, both reaching the target to $\pm0.01\%$ ---
while the SSM backbone costs $\sim\!90\times$ less per evaluation
(Fig.~\ref{fig:svse}). This is the intended division of labour: the SSM adjoint does
the search cheaply, and full-order EPMC confirms the optimum.

\subsection{EPMC- vs SSM-based optimization: differentiation, cost, and robustness}
\label{sec:prosncons}
The head-to-head hides a structural difference that decides which engine is usable
\emph{at scale}. It turns on how the gradient is obtained, how cost scales with the
number of design variables $m$, and how smooth the objective is --- not on which
backbone is more accurate (EPMC is, by construction).

\paragraph{Why derivative-free is possible, and why it does not scale.}
Optimizing the EPMC backbone requires only the ability to \emph{evaluate} it: for
any design one runs the continuation and reads a backbone point, so any zeroth-order
method (golden-section/Brent, Nelder--Mead, pattern or mesh-adaptive search) applies
without ever forming a gradient --- exactly the $1$-D bracketing search used in
Sec.~\ref{sec:epmc}, which recomputes the full backbone at $\sim\!10$ trial
curvatures. The limitation is dimensional: a derivative-free search over $m$
variables needs at least $m{+}1$ evaluations to span the space and, in practice,
$\mathcal O(m)$--$\mathcal O(m^{2})$ per improving step, \emph{each a full
continuation}. For one variable that is $\sim\!10$ continuations; for a tow-angle
field ($m\sim10^{2}$--$10^{3}$) it is millions. Derivative-free EPMC optimization is
thus a tool for a handful of variables only, whereas the SSM (and the EPMC
\emph{adjoint}, Eqs.~\eqref{eq:epmc-adj}--\eqref{eq:epmc-dtheta}) obtain the entire
gradient at a cost independent of $m$.

\paragraph{Smoothness and robustness of the objective.}
The SSM objective is an \emph{analytic} function of the design --- the reduced
coefficients $\gamma_{2k+1}(\theta)$ are smooth compositions of the eigenpairs and
the multilinear forms --- so the backbone and its gradient vary smoothly and
gradient descent is well conditioned (until the SSM itself loses validity). The EPMC
objective is a point read from a \emph{numerical continuation}, and it can be
non-smooth or even discontinuous in the design wherever the continuation \emph{folds}
(turning points, where the frequency at a fixed amplitude becomes multivalued), where
the nonlinear mode \emph{bifurcates} (an internal resonance splits the branch), or
where a \emph{mode-jump}/snap-through changes which branch is primary --- precisely
the pathologies we met steering into the deep-arch regime. Across these features
gradients are undefined or misleading and zeroth-order methods see cliffs; robust
EPMC optimization needs branch tracking, fold/bifurcation detection, and careful
point selection, machinery the smooth SSM landscape never requires.

\paragraph{Validity and locality.}
Conversely, the SSM is an \emph{asymptotic} model built at one equilibrium: exact in
the leading coefficient but truncation-limited to a convergence radius in amplitude,
and blind to isolated branches (isolas) and to global bifurcation structure away from
the equilibrium. EPMC is \emph{exact} at every amplitude the continuation reaches ---
capturing folds, the large-amplitude softening/hardening reversal, and internal
resonances directly --- and it traces the \emph{global} branch. Where the objective
lives beyond the SSM radius or involves the global response, EPMC (or its
forced-response form) is not optional.

\paragraph{Recommendation.}
The two are complementary and the division of labour is clean (Tab.~\ref{tab:prosncons}):
the \textbf{SSM adjoint} does the search --- cheap, smooth, and with a gradient whose
cost is independent of $m$, the only option at field scale --- and \textbf{full-order
EPMC} validates the optimum and designs in the regimes the SSM cannot reach
(large amplitude, internal resonance, folds), warm-started from the SSM design. Both
share the identical $\dd\bar{\bm Q}/\dd\theta$ chain (\S\ref{sec:epmc}), so the hybrid
costs no extra sensitivity code.

\subsection{Forced-response objectives, and the validity boundary}
\label{sec:handoff}
Two extensions decide how far the comparison reaches. First, most design targets live
on the \emph{forced} response, not the backbone --- a peak amplitude at a given drive,
the up- and down-jump frequencies, the saddle-node spacing, or whether a detached
response branch (an isola) exists. Both engines carry over, and the tradeoff is
unchanged. On the SSM side the leading-order forced response is algebraic in the
\emph{same} reduced coefficients (\S\ref{sec:plate}),
$[(\Om-\Om(\rho))^2+\alpha(\rho)^2]\,\rho^2=|f_1|^2$, so peak amplitude, jump locations
and their design sensitivities follow from the same adjoint at no extra model cost ---
cheap and smooth, but inheriting the amplitude radius and, being \emph{local}, blind to
isolas detached from the main branch. On the EPMC side one replaces the self-excitation
in Eq.~\eqref{eq:epmc-res} by the physical forcing and continues in frequency; the
resulting harmonic-balance response curve is exact --- folds, jumps and isolas included
--- and its design sensitivity is the \emph{same} implicit-differentiation adjoint
(Eqs.~\eqref{eq:epmc-adj}--\eqref{eq:epmc-dtheta}) built around the forced residual. The
one qualitative addition: an isola is invisible to a local reduced model but is found by
global continuation, so objectives that turn on isola birth or death are an EPMC (or
forced-harmonic-balance) domain. There is also a subtlety the smoothness analysis
predicts --- a peak-amplitude objective is smooth, but a \emph{jump-frequency} objective
is the location of a fold, a bifurcation, so it is non-smooth in the design and needs the
fold-tracking machinery of \S\ref{sec:prosncons}.

Second, the SSM's validity is bounded and the handoff is quantifiable. On our plate the
cubic SSM backbone tracks full-order EPMC to $\lesssim\!2\%$ up to $a/h\approx0.3$ and
then over-hardens --- $12.5\%$ versus the EPMC $10.5\%$ relative detuning at $a/h=0.4$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:epmcssm}), a $\sim\!19\%$ error where the true backbone's quartic term
bends below the cubic parabola. Objectives that live at $a/h\lesssim0.3$ are therefore
safely designed on the cubic SSM; beyond that one raises the SSM order (the quintic
$\gamma_5$ captures the turn-over) or hands the design to EPMC. Geometrically this is
the SSM's radius of validity: the reduction is a polynomial \emph{graph} over the master
eigenplane (Fig.~\ref{fig:manifold}), accurate in a neighbourhood of the equilibrium,
whereas EPMC's continuation traces the exact invariant surface as far out as it stays
connected. The rule of thumb is the whole comparison in one line: \emph{design on the
SSM within its verified radius; verify, and if needed finish, on EPMC.}

\section{Discussion and comparison to prior work}

Our reduction and optimization loop are the same class as
\cite{pozzi2025,liang2025,xiao2026}; the departure is the design variable and the
constraint. Where those works tune \emph{shape/size/density}, we tune the
\emph{fiber-orientation field}, which for a composite is the natural
manufacturing degree of freedom and which --- through the bending--extension
coupling --- acts directly on the nonlinearity, not only on the linear stiffness.
Where the variable-stiffness-composite literature
\cite{gurdal,ijsselmuiden} steers tows for \emph{linear} objectives (stiffness,
buckling, linear frequency) under a tow-curvature limit, we carry the same
manufacturability constraint onto the \emph{nonlinear} backbone. The combination
--- fiber steering for nonlinear-dynamic tailoring, subject to manufacturability
--- appears to be new.

The main limitation is physical and we state it plainly: on a clamped beam the
coupling lever modulates but does not dominate the strong membrane hardening, so
pure steering improves amplitude-robustness by a bounded amount rather than
achieving iso-frequency. This is a property of the structure and the bounded
coupling, not of the method; the softening-reserve routes above, and a move to a
two-dimensional laminated plate (where a steered fiber \emph{field} has far more
freedom and where the adjoint sensitivity of \cite{pozzi2025} becomes essential),
are the clear paths to a larger effect.

\section{Conclusion}
We have made a composite's nonlinear backbone a function of its manufacturable
tow path, reduced it on a verified arbitrary-order spectral submanifold, and
optimized the tow path for amplitude-robustness under a tow-curvature constraint.
On a clamped beam the lever is the bending--extension coupling of an unsymmetric,
steered laminate --- it is the steering, not merely the layup, that supplies the
tunable nonlinearity --- and it is real but bounded, which we quantify honestly.
On a two-dimensional laminated plate the anisotropic membrane stiffness itself
becomes the knob and the effect is far stronger: a curl-feasible steered field
halves the amplitude detuning at held linear frequency, a figure confirmed by the
SSM backbone, full-order EPMC, and brute-force time-domain sweeps. The engineering
consequence is the point (Sec.~\ref{sec:utility}): the steering removes the
forced-response jump and widens the panel's jump- and hysteresis-free operating
range by $1.42\times$ ($\sim\!2.4\times$ with an added topology field), the
amplitude-robustness a resonant sensor, vibration harvester, or hysteresis-free
actuator actually needs --- reshaping the \emph{nonlinear} response at fixed linear
frequency and fixed mass, a lever no classical composite knob provides. The
framework extends directly to forced-response objectives and, through the
field-scale EPMC adjoint, to full finite-element models.

\texttt{nltopt} code (depending on the \texttt{PyHB} harmonic-balance/EPMC
library and borrowing the manufacturability machinery of \texttt{wavetopo});
the verification suite and the figure scripts are included.

\appendix
\section{Computation of the SSM coefficients}\label{app:coeffs}

This appendix gives the exact recursion that produces every coefficient
$\bm w_{\bm m}$ (the geometry) and $\gamma_{2k+1}$ (the reduced dynamics) used in
the paper, matching the implementation line for line.

\subsection{Master spectral data}
Cast~\eqref{eq:eom} in first order, $\bm{\mathcal B}\dot{\bm z}=\bm{\mathcal A}\bm z
+\bm F(\bm z)$, with $\bm{\mathcal A},\bm{\mathcal B}$ as in~\eqref{eq:firstorder}.
Solve the linear generalized eigenproblem $\bm K\bm\varphi=\omega_0^2\bm M\bm\varphi$,
mass-normalize $\bm\varphi^{\!\top}\bm M\bm\varphi=1$, and set
$\zeta_0=\bm\varphi^{\!\top}\bm C\bm\varphi/(2\omega_0)$. The master pencil eigenpair
solves $\bm{\mathcal A}\bm v_1=\lambda_1\bm{\mathcal B}\bm v_1$ with
$\lambda_1=-\zeta_0\omega_0+i\omega_0\sqrt{1-\zeta_0^2}$; scale $\bm v_1$ so its
displacement block is $\bm\varphi$. Take the left eigenvector $\bm u_1$
($\bm u_1^{*}\bm{\mathcal A}=\lambda_1\bm u_1^{*}\bm{\mathcal B}$), normalized
$\bm u_1^{*}\bm{\mathcal B}\bm v_1=1$, and set $\bm v_2=\bar{\bm v}_1$,
$\lambda_2=\bar\lambda_1$. Seed the coefficient tables at first order:
$\bm w_{(1,0)}=\bm v_1$, $\bm w_{(0,1)}=\bm v_2$, $R^1_{(1,0)}=\lambda_1$,
$R^2_{(0,1)}=\lambda_2$.

\subsection{Right-hand side at each order}
For a multi-index $\bm m=(m_1,m_2)$, $|\bm m|\ge2$, the homological
equation~\eqref{eq:homological} has right-hand side
$\bm G_{\bm m}=\bm G^{\mathrm{nl}}_{\bm m}-\bm G^{\mathrm{cr}}_{\bm m}$, both assembled
from \emph{lower} orders. The nonlinear part is the order-$\bm m$ term of
$-\bm F(\bm W(\bm p))$, a multi-index convolution of the displacement blocks
$\bm w^{x}$ over all non-trivial partitions,
\begin{equation}
\bm G^{\mathrm{nl}}_{\bm m}=-\!\!\!\sum_{\substack{\bm a+\bm b=\bm m\\ \bm a,\bm b\ne\bm 0}}\!\!\!
\begin{bmatrix}\bm B_2(\bm w^{x}_{\bm a},\bm w^{x}_{\bm b})\\ \bm 0\end{bmatrix}
-\!\!\!\!\sum_{\substack{\bm a+\bm b+\bm c=\bm m\\ \bm a,\bm b,\bm c\ne\bm 0}}\!\!\!\!
\begin{bmatrix}\bm T_3(\bm w^{x}_{\bm a},\bm w^{x}_{\bm b},\bm w^{x}_{\bm c})\\ \bm 0\end{bmatrix},
\label{eq:app-forcing}
\end{equation}
and the cross-term part is what $\mathrm D\bm W\,\bm R$ already contributes from known
nonlinear reduced coefficients,
$\bm G^{\mathrm{cr}}_{\bm m}=\bm{\mathcal B}\sum_{j=1,2}\sum_{\bm a+\bm e_j=\bm m,\,|\bm b|\ge2}
a_j\,\bm w_{\bm a}R^{j}_{\bm b}$, evaluated over the already-computed $\bm w,R$.

\subsection{Solving each order}
Sweep $q=2,\dots,\text{order}$, and within each $q$ the indices $\bm m=(m_1,m_2)$ with
$m_1\ge m_2$ (the conjugate partner is filled by symmetry). Put
$\Lambda_{\bm m}=m_1\lambda_1+m_2\lambda_2$. Two cases:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Near-resonant} ($m_1-m_2=1$, i.e.\ $\bm m=(k{+}1,k)$, where
$\Lambda_{\bm m}=\lambda_1$ conservatively): the operator $\Lambda_{\bm m}\bm{\mathcal B}
-\bm{\mathcal A}$ is (near-)singular along $\bm v_1$. Project onto the left eigenvector
to read the reduced coefficient, then solve the deflated system for the geometry under
the normal-form gauge $\bm u_1^{*}\bm{\mathcal B}\bm w_{\bm m}=0$:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;\gamma_{2k+1}=R^1_{\bm m}=\bm u_1^{*}\bm G_{\bm m}\;},\qquad
(\Lambda_{\bm m}\bm{\mathcal B}-\bm{\mathcal A})\,\bm w_{\bm m}
=\bm G_{\bm m}-\bm{\mathcal B}\bm v_1\,R^1_{\bm m},
\label{eq:app-gamma}
\end{equation}
followed by $\bm w_{\bm m}\!\leftarrow\!\bm w_{\bm m}-(\bm u_1^{*}\bm{\mathcal B}\bm w_{\bm m})\bm v_1$.
Under light damping $\Lambda_{\bm m}\ne\lambda_1$ exactly and the solve is nonsingular; at
exact resonance one uses the least-squares pseudo-inverse and the same deflation.
\item \textbf{Non-resonant}: set $R_{\bm m}=\bm 0$ and invert directly,
$\bm w_{\bm m}=(\Lambda_{\bm m}\bm{\mathcal B}-\bm{\mathcal A})^{-1}\bm G_{\bm m}$.
\end{itemize}
The reduced coefficients are $\{\gamma_{2k+1}\}=\{R^1_{(k+1,k)}\}$ ($\gamma_3$ at
$k{=}1$, $\gamma_5$ at $k{=}2$, \dots), and the backbone is~\eqref{eq:backbone}.

\subsection{Worked coefficient: the cubic term}
At order 3 the only near-resonant index is $\bm m=(2,1)$. By~\eqref{eq:app-forcing} its
forcing is the cubic force on the master eigenvectors plus the quadratic acting on the
order-2 map, $\bm G_{(2,1)}=-\big[\,3\,\bm T_3(\bm v_1,\bm v_1,\bar{\bm v}_1)
+2\bm B_2(\bm v_1,\bm w_{(1,1)})+\bm B_2(\bar{\bm v}_1,\bm w_{(2,0)})\,\big]$ (displacement
block), and \eqref{eq:app-gamma} gives $\gamma_3=\bm u_1^{*}\bm G_{(2,1)}$. For a
single-DOF Duffing $\ddot x+\omega_0^2x+\varepsilon x^3=0$ the quadratic vanishes, the
order-2 map is zero, and $\gamma_3$ reduces to the classical
$\Om=\omega_0\big(1+\tfrac38(\varepsilon/\omega_0^2)A^2\big)$. For the von~K\'arm\'an
plate the membrane coupling $\bm B_2$ (the $u\!\leftrightarrow\!w^2$ term) is nonzero, its
order-2 slaving $\bm w_{(1,1)},\bm w_{(2,0)}$ enters through $\bm B_2$ above, and
$\gamma_3$ is exactly the relaxed cubic $\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}$ of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:gammaeff}: the direct membrane cubic \emph{minus} the in-plane relaxation.
This is why Eq.~\eqref{eq:gamma}, $\gamma_{2k+1}=\bm u_1^{*}\bm G_{(k+1,k)}$, is already an
adjoint projection --- and why its design sensitivity is inexpensive.

\section{Finite-element validation of the assumed-modes discretization}\label{app:fe}

\paragraph{Why this is needed.} The reductions above are verified against the analytic
Duffing backbone and against full-order EPMC and direct time integration. Those
confirmations, however, are of the \emph{same} assumed-modes system solved at higher
fidelity: they validate the SSM \emph{reduction}, not the assumed-modes
\emph{discretization}. A steered laminate is spatially heterogeneous and, off the
principal axes, develops bend--twist couplings $D_{16},D_{26}$; whether a handful of
global sine modes resolve its response is a separate question. We settle it with an
independent finite-element (FE) model and the identical backbone coefficient.

\subsection{FE model}
We take a flat, symmetric ($\bm B=\bm0$) plate on a uniform $n_x\times n_y$ rectangular
mesh, bending and membrane discretized independently (they decouple linearly for
$\bm B=\bm 0$).

\emph{Bending (Kirchhoff).} The non-conforming 12-DOF ACM rectangular element carries
nodal degrees of freedom $\bm d_e=\{w,\;w_{,y},\;-w_{,x}\}$ at the four corners. On the
element (local $s,t\in[0,1]$) the deflection is the 12-term polynomial
\begin{equation}
w(s,t)=\bm p^{\!\top}\bm a,\quad
\bm p=[1,s,t,s^2,st,t^2,s^3,s^2t,st^2,t^3,s^3t,st^3]^{\!\top};
\end{equation}
enforcing the nodal DOF gives $\bm d_e=\bm C\bm a\Rightarrow\bm a=\bm C^{-1}\bm d_e$. With
the curvature $\bm\kappa=[w_{,xx},w_{,yy},2w_{,xy}]^{\!\top}=\bm B_b\bm C^{-1}\bm d_e$
($\bm B_b$ the side-scaled second derivatives of $\bm p$), and $4\times4$ Gauss quadrature,
\begin{equation}
\bm K^b_e=\!\int_{\Omega_e}\!\bm C^{-\top}\bm B_b^{\!\top}\,\bm D(\theta_e)\,\bm B_b\bm C^{-1}\,\dd\Omega,
\quad
\bm M^b_e=\rho h\!\int_{\Omega_e}\!\bm C^{-\top}\bm p\bm p^{\!\top}\bm C^{-1}\,\dd\Omega,
\end{equation}
$\bm D(\theta_e)=\tfrac{h^3}{12}\bar{\bm Q}(\theta_e)$ evaluated per element, so
$D_{16},D_{26}$ are retained exactly.

\emph{Membrane (Q4).} The in-plane fields use the 8-DOF bilinear quadrilateral; with the
membrane strain--displacement $\bm B_m$ and $\bm A(\theta_e)=h\,\bar{\bm Q}(\theta_e)$,
$\bm K^{\mathrm{in}}_e=\int_{\Omega_e}\bm B_m^{\!\top}\bm A(\theta_e)\bm B_m\,\dd\Omega$.

\emph{Boundary conditions.} Navier simple support: $w=0$ on the boundary (bending) and
$u=v=0$ on the boundary (membrane) --- matching the sine in-plane modes, which vanish on
the edges. \emph{Self-validation:} for a specially-orthotropic UD $[0]$ plate the FE
reproduces the closed form
$\omega_{mn}=\pi^2\sqrt{[D_{11}(m/a)^4+2(D_{12}{+}2D_{66})(m/a)^2(n/b)^2+D_{22}(n/b)^4]/\rho h}$
to $f_1=256.0$ vs.\ $256.3$~Hz, converging monotonically with mesh
(\texttt{examples/plate\_fe\_check.py}).

\subsection{Backbone by static condensation on the mesh}
We compute the \emph{same} backbone coefficient the plate results use ---
Eq.~\eqref{eq:gammaeff} --- but FE-discretized. Solve $\bm K^b\bm\varphi=\omega_1^2\bm M^b\bm\varphi$
for the fundamental mode (unit peak deflection); its von~K\'arm\'an membrane strain is
$\bm\varepsilon_{NL}=[\tfrac12 w_{,x}^2,\tfrac12 w_{,y}^2,w_{,x}w_{,y}]^{\!\top}$, with the
gradients read off the ACM element. Assembling
$\bm g=\!\int\bm B_m^{\!\top}\bm A\,\bm\varepsilon_{NL}$,
$\mathrm{knl}=2\!\int\bm\varepsilon_{NL}^{\!\top}\bm A\,\bm\varepsilon_{NL}$ and
$K_b=\bm\varphi^{\!\top}\bm K^b\bm\varphi$, static condensation of the fast in-plane fields gives
\begin{equation}
\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}=\mathrm{knl}-2\,\bm g^{\!\top}(\bm K^{\mathrm{in}})^{-1}\bm g,
\qquad
\Om(a)/\omega_1=1+\beta\,(a/h)^2,\quad \beta=\tfrac38\,(\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}/K_b)\,h^2 .
\end{equation}
This costs \emph{one} sparse solve --- no continuation or time integration --- because the
condensation \emph{is} the order-2 SSM slaving (App.~\ref{app:coeffs}) evaluated on the mesh.
The identical formula on the sine basis returns the assumed-modes backbone, so any difference
is purely discretization (\texttt{examples/plate\_fe\_backbone.py}).

Two conclusions. \emph{(i)} For a homogeneous laminate the FE and assumed-modes backbones
agree to $1\%$, validating the FE nonlinear model (membrane, coupling, condensation).
\emph{(ii)} For strong steering the assumed-modes frequency is a Ritz upper bound that
converges slowly, but the \emph{normalized backbone detuning} $\beta\propto\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}/K_b$
is far more robust --- a ratio of two integrals of the \emph{same} slightly-inaccurate mode, so
the mode-shape error largely cancels, leaving a $2\%$ discrepancy at $24$ modes. The relative
detuning results the designs are built on are therefore FE-accurate to a few percent; absolute
frequencies of strongly steered designs should be read from FE or an enriched
($\gtrsim24$-mode) expansion. A displacement-based von~K\'arm\'an FE would replace the assumed
modes throughout at higher cost, reduced by the same SSM.
